# Introduction

Two deterministic paradigms dominate light-water-reactor core calculations. *Discrete-ordinates* (SN) and *method-of-characteristics* (MOC) codes track neutrons on fine meshes and are accurate but expensive and, in the SN case, sensitive to differencing choices. *Nodal* diffusion codes work on coarse meshes and are cheap and robust, but classical diffusion under-predicts the transport corrections (shielding, pin-wise self shielding) that control $\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and power shape.

The **Phase Space Nodal (PSN)** method of Chao *et al.* [@psn2027] bridges the two. Within each node the scalar flux is represented by a separable hyperbolic profile in the two axes, plus a five-term quadratic particular solution of the source; the angular flux is reconstructed from the spatial field as $4\pi\psi(\mathbf{r},\boldsymbol{\Omega})
=\phi(\mathbf{r})-\Sigma_t^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla\phi
(\mathbf{r})$, so its directional variation is carried by a small set of phase-space (angular) moments with closed-form correction coefficients. The node is eliminated to give explicit relations between surface currents and node sources, and neighbouring nodes are coupled through those currents. The result is a nodal code with a compact spatial representation; the present implementation uses a sparse assembly of the node-coupling system rather than the dense form of the original reference code.

Local angular expansions on subdomains of the direction domain precede the present work. Ghazaie *et al.* formulated a Multi-PN approximation using separate angular expansions on subdomains and a finite-element treatment of space [@ghazaie2019mpn]; Falabino *et al.* developed a piecewise-polynomial representation of interface angular fluxes within a short-characteristic transport solver [@falabino2022mpn]. Chao *et al.* distinguish their PSN construction from such independently selected local angular expansions [@psn2027]: in PSN, the angular structure is generated by powers of the streaming operator acting on spatial functions governed by diffusion-type equations, and their proposed SP3-based development concerns higher streaming-operator powers rather than a higher polynomial degree in azimuth.

The contributions of this work are fivefold. First, we implement the published PSN formulation in a multigroup framework with rectangular nodes, sparse assembly and vectorised local operators. Second, we verify the original branch through the reported implementation checks and the reproduction of the published checkerboard and BWR-bundle results. Third, we document a separate segmentwise even-parity angular-moment branch, including its matrix-valued nodal response and its relation to the original PSN closure. Fourth, we document a finite-sector closure of the same even-parity hierarchy, in which the diffusion tensor of each finite angular cell is the exact second moment of the retained directions and the interface and vacuum terms come from the same weak form as the body term; on the square checkerboard that closure removes the sign-changing diagonal valley of the published error survey over every tested point. Fifth, we assess the available checkerboard and C5G7-based calculations using references appropriate to each material and geometry model. The assessment preserves the distinction between eigenvalue trends and spatial-field accuracy, and between controlled comparisons and comparisons in which both the angular formulation and pin tiling change. We do not claim a new principle of local angular expansion or a general accuracy or efficiency ranking against the cited methods.

The remainder is organised as follows. Section 2 describes the formulations and their implementation: the legacy PSN formulation, the rectangular-node implementation, the separate segmentwise angular-moment formulation, and the finite-sector closure of the even-parity equation. Section 3 presents the implementation verification and the reproduction of the published PSN results. Section 4 assesses the checkerboard and C5G7-based calculations, distinguishing the pin-homogenised and explicit-material models and the two formulations: the legacy PSN branch on the $3\times3$ pin tiling (homogenised and explicit rectangular nodes), the segmentwise angular-moment variant at $A{=}3$ on the $4\times4$ fuel-split tiling, and the finite-sector closure on the same four families of benchmarks, with fission-field comparisons against OpenMC. Section 5 discusses the scope of the comparisons and the relation to prior methods; Section 6 gives the conclusions. Appendix A tabulates the full point-by-point reproduction of the published results.

# Numerical formulations and implementation

OpenPSN is a single software framework containing two distinct formulations of the nodal transport problem. The first is an implementation of the published legacy PSN construction [@psn2027], including a rectangular-node extension. The second is a separate segmentwise even-parity angular-moment variant with a PSN-inspired nodal response, assessed at angular order $A{=}3$. The two share the rectangular geometry, the multi-group assembly and the outer fission-source iteration; they differ in the angular closure and, in the C5G7 campaigns, in the pin tiling. The second branch also carries a finite-sector closure of its own, selected by `angular.closure: sector_average` or `sector_diffusion` and assessed in Section 4. The software name OpenPSN does not imply that all of its results are instances of the original PSN method.

## Legacy PSN

The full theory is given in Ref. [@psn2027]; we summarise the ingredients needed here. The legacy PSN reconstruction combines a spatial nodal field with a direction-dependent gradient term: the scalar flux is a separable hyperbolic profile in space (plus the five-term source particular solution) whose coefficients are fixed by the node-averaged flux and a set of surface-current (moment) unknowns, and the angular flux is reconstructed from that field, so it is *not* constant within an angular segment --- its directional variation is constrained by derivatives of the underlying spatial field. Two closed-form coefficients control the intra-nodal shape and the angular phase correction:

$$\begin{align}
\alpha_{imn} &\quad\text{(face-flux weight, }\overline{\Phi}_{imn}
=\alpha_{imn}\,\overline{\phi}_{im}^{\,n}\text{, Eq.~(2.8a) of
Ref.~\cite{psn2027}),}\\
\beta_i  &= 1-\tfrac{1}{4}\cos\Delta\theta_i-\tfrac{1}{4}\cos 2\theta_i
          -\tfrac{1}{2}\cos 2\theta_i\,\cos\Delta\theta_i
          \quad\text{(generic phase-space coefficient, Eq.~2.8d).}
\end{align}$$

The closed form for $\beta_i$ in Eq. (2.8d) is an equivalent closed-form expression re-derived from the integral form of the phase-space moment, and the two are algebraically identical; we verified the two to $2.2\times10^{-16}$ and use the closed form in the implementation. In the fine-angle limit the coefficient reduces to the single-direction P1 closure $(3/2)\sin^2\theta_i$.

Eliminating the node unknowns yields a sparse system coupling the surface-current (and node-source) unknowns of neighbouring nodes, which is iterated with a standard fission-source (power) iteration; the sparse node-coupling system is solved directly at each outer sweep.

## Implementation and the high-precision formulation

OpenPSN (run as `python -m psn2d run problem.yaml`) is a pure-Python implementation (NumPy/SciPy) accepting any number of energy groups, an arbitrary material grid with independent per-edge reflect/vacuum boundary conditions, and a user-set angular order $M$, spatial sub-division $S$, angular model, and convergence tolerance. The four implementation components are:

1.  **Sparse assembly (removes the quadratic growth).** For the legacy PSN branch the nodal coupling matrix has only $\sim$`<!-- -->`{=html}9 non-zeroes per row (the node couples to its own face-current unknowns and to its neighbours), so the assembly scales linearly in the node count where the natural dense form scales quadratically. We assemble in coordinate (COO) format and solve in compressed-sparse-column (CSC) format; the path agrees with the plain dense reference to $10^{-15}$, and the $10{,}404$-node refinement ($250{,}920$ unknowns) converges to $10^{-10}$ in the relative eigenvalue change. The angular-moment branch assembles a larger per-node system ($4K$ face-mode unknowns at $A{=}3$) with the same sparse structure.

2.  **Vectorised node operators, verified to $10^{-15}$.** The node-state map is strictly linear in the $(J_4,q)$ unknowns. We probe it with nine basis vectors once to build the $9\times9$ linear map, then apply it as a batched matrix multiply over all nodes and all groups. The vectorised path is verified identical to the validated scalar loop to $10^{-15}$ on five representative problems (the shipped verification script `verify_vectorized_equiv.py`): it changes no number of the validated solver.

3.  **Rectangular nodes with per-node widths (no homogenisation).** The original formulation carries a single material per node, with no intra-node structure, so a pin cell enters either as a homogenised node or through an explicit multi-node geometry. OpenPSN extends the node operators to arbitrary per-node widths: each rectangular node carries its own width in $x$ and $y$, and nodes meet corner-to-corner only (no T-junctions). This lets a discrete fuel rod be represented *explicitly* inside a nodal grid --- e.g. the C5G7 1.26 cm circular rod is replaced by an equal-area $s=\sqrt{\pi r^{2}}=0.95713$ cm fuel square centred on the same pitch, giving a $3\times3$ rectangle per pin and a globally edge-aligned tiling (17$\times$`<!-- -->`{=html}17 assembly $=51\times51$ nodes; 1/4 core $=153\times153$). Un-homogenised 7-group cross sections can then be assigned per rectangle (fuel vs. water), and the homogenisation model --- the usual source of nodal bias on MOX benchmarks --- is removed from the problem entirely. The rectangular node operators are the same closed-form parabolic/phase-space forms as the square case evaluated on the actual node widths; they are verified node-by-node against the square-node path (which they reduce to exactly when all widths are equal).

4.  **Segmentwise angular-moment nodal variant (Section [2.3](#sec:amvariant){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:amvariant"}).** The additional angular-moment branch retains $K{=}A{+}1$ local even-parity modes per segment. It uses a PSN-inspired nodal response, but changes the angular closure and the body decay scales. The original-scale $A{=}0$ regression branch and the directional $A{=}0$ member must be distinguished; only the former is reported to reproduce the legacy state matrix, and $A{=}1,2,3$ increase the local angular polynomial order. Each $(i,g)$ node system is a fixed sparse linear system in $K$ face-mode unknowns per face ($4K$ per node), and every point of the assessment converges to the same $10^{-7}$ gate. The accuracy of the local assembly is certified against an independent full-mesh direct solve: at full convergence the eigenvalues agree to ${\le}2\times10^{-14}$ for $A{=}1,2,3$.

These components are shared by both branches where stated: they make the published method reproducible to machine precision and let each branch converge to $10^{-7}$--$10^{-10}$ in relative eigenvalue change on multi-group coarse cores, at subdivisions the dense formulation cannot address. The physics of the two branches differs, however: the angular-moment variant changes the angular closure, the body decay scales and the per-node unknown count, and that change is the subject of the assessment in Section 4 rather than an implementation convenience.

## The segmentwise angular-moment variant {#sec:amvariant}

### The $A{=}3$ closure: projection of the even-parity transport equation, compared with PSN

The local angular-moment branch changes the retained angular space and the body closure. It starts from the even-parity form of the transport equation under the stated material and source assumptions, represents the even field within each azimuthal segment in local Legendre modes, and reconstructs the odd field from the even-field gradient. Its sectorwise moment construction is classified here as multi-$P_N$-type; its use of a PSN-inspired spatial response does not make it the original PSN closure at a higher angular order. In particular, $A{=}3$ denotes a cubic polynomial in local azimuth with four retained even coefficients per segment; it does not denote global spherical-harmonic $P_3$ or SP$_3$. The polar treatment remains the one stated for each calculation.

*Where the original PSN approximates.* Start from the steady multi-group transport equation inside a homogeneous node of the original phase-space nodal method [@psn2027], Eq. (2.1a) of that paper: $$\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla\psi(\boldsymbol{\Omega},\mathbf{r})
+\Sigma_t\psi(\boldsymbol{\Omega},\mathbf{r}) = \frac{Q(\mathbf{r})}{4\pi},
\label{eq:transport}
\end{equation}$$ with $Q$ the isotropic (scattering $+$ fission) source and $\phi=\int\psi\,d\Omega$. The lowest GSPN ansatz, Eq. (2.3a) of Ref. [@psn2027], $$\begin{equation}
\psi_{P_1} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\Big[\phi_0
-\frac{1}{\Sigma_t}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla\phi_0\Big],
\end{equation}$$ annihilates the first-derivative term when substituted back into Eq. [\[eq:transport\]](#eq:transport){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:transport"}, leaving $-\frac{1}{\Sigma_t}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla)^2\phi_0
+\Sigma_t\phi_0 = Q$, which no single angle-independent $\phi_0$ can satisfy for every direction. The original paper therefore integrates over the full solid angle, using $\langle\Omega_j\Omega_l\rangle_{4\pi}=\delta_{jl}/3$, and obtains the isotropic diffusion equation, its Eq. (2.3c), $$\begin{equation}
-\frac{1}{3\Sigma_t}\nabla^2\phi_0+\Sigma_t\phi_0 = Q,
\label{eq:diff3c}
\end{equation}$$ whose wave number is $k^2 = 3\Sigma_t^2$ (Eq. (2.11b)). The reconstruction $\psi = \frac{1}{4\pi}[\phi - \Sigma_t^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla\phi]$ with $\phi=\phi_0+C$ (Eqs. (2.3d,e)) adds one node-constant degree of freedom fixed by the phase-space node balance (Eq. (2.9a)), but the *body* of the node is still governed by the full-solid-angle average of Eq. [\[eq:diff3c\]](#eq:diff3c){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:diff3c"}: the direction-dependent operator $(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla)^2$ has been replaced by the angle-averaged Laplacian. The face quantities, by contrast, retain the true angular second moments: the original face current (Eq. (2.8b)) carries the polar factor $\beta_i/3=\tau_i/2$ with $\beta_i=\tfrac32\,\tau_i$ (Eq. (2.8d)) and the azimuthal chordal-averaging factor $\gamma_m=\sin\Delta\varphi_m/\Delta\varphi_m$, i.e.\
a direction-dependent diffusion tensor, with $c_m$ the segment centre, $$D^{(0)}_{im} = \frac{\tau_i}{2\Sigma_t}
\begin{pmatrix}
1+\gamma_m\cos 2c_m & \gamma_m\sin 2c_m\\
\gamma_m\sin 2c_m & 1-\gamma_m\cos 2c_m
\end{pmatrix}$$ with $\tau_i=\langle\sin^2\theta\rangle_i$. **The original method thus evaluates the body on the isotropic scale while the faces carry the anisotropic angular moments; the $A{=}3$ angular-moment closure makes the body use the same angular second moments as the faces, at every angular order.**

*Parity elimination and the exact even-parity equation.* Write $f=4\pi\psi$ and split into even and odd parity, $e(\Omega)=\tfrac12[f(\Omega)+f(-\Omega)]$, $o(\Omega)=\tfrac12[f(\Omega)-f(-\Omega)]$: $\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla o+\Sigma_t e=Q$ and $\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla e+\Sigma_t o=0$. Inside a node of constant $\Sigma_t$ the odd parity is eliminated *exactly*, $o=-\Sigma_t^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla e$, giving the directional second-order equation $$\begin{equation}
-\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla\Big(\Sigma_t^{-1}
\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla e\Big)+\Sigma_t e = Q.
\label{eq:A1}
\end{equation}$$ This is the mother equation of the closure. If the even-parity trial field is restricted to be angle-independent and Eq. [\[eq:A1\]](#eq:A1){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:A1"} is then averaged over the full solid angle, $\langle\boldsymbol{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\rangle_{4\pi}
=\tfrac13\mathbf{I}$, the result is Eq. [\[eq:diff3c\]](#eq:diff3c){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:diff3c"}, and the original reconstruction (2.3a) is recovered as the exact parity-elimination identity. Odd-parity elimination is performed *before* angular projection, so the projected streaming terms contain the *full* second angular moments $\langle b_a\,\Omega_x^2\, b_b\rangle$. If both parities were first projected onto the same finite angular space, eliminating the projected odd field would generally produce products of projected first moments, $\langle b_a\Omega_x b_c\rangle\langle b_c\Omega_x b_b\rangle$, instead; these constructions need not coincide. This observation does not distinguish the present equations from all multi-$P_N$ formulations: an appropriate odd space containing the streamed even trial functions can yield the same second-order moment system after elimination. The even-parity form is used here to define the chosen closure, without a novelty claim for parity elimination itself. The eliminated odd flux is reconstructable from $\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla e$ and is not required to stay inside the truncated angular space.

*Polar reduction and the two polar moments.* For a $z$-invariant 2-D problem the unit direction keeps its three components $\boldsymbol{\Omega}=(\sin\theta\cos\varphi,
\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\cos\theta)$ and the operator uses $\partial_z=0$; with even-parity pairing over opposite directions the $z$-component drops out of every even-parity moment below, so only the planar projection $\boldsymbol{\Omega}_\perp=(\sin\theta\cos\varphi,
\sin\theta\sin\varphi,0)$ enters. The scalar flux is the half-plane average $\phi=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^\pi e\,d\varphi\,d\zeta$, $\zeta=\cos\theta$; this half-plane folding uses, beyond the even-parity pairing, reflection symmetry of the source and the boundaries in $z$ (or an equivalent mirrored $z$-folded field) --- the isotropic-source, vacuum/mirror 2-D configurations of this paper satisfy it, and the even-parity pairing alone does not extend to arbitrary asymmetric incident boundaries. Two polar moments are carried per polar sector $i$: the folded solid-angle weight $W_i=\int_{\theta_-}^{\theta_+}\sin\theta\,d\theta$ (normalized, $\sum_i W_i=1$), the second moment $\tau_i=\langle\sin^2\theta\rangle_i$ and the first moment $\kappa_i=\langle\sin\theta\rangle_i$. With the generic even $\theta$-partition $\Delta\theta=\pi/I$, $$\begin{align}
\tau_i &= 1-\frac{\zeta_+^2+\zeta_+\zeta_-+\zeta_-^2}{3}, \\
\kappa_i &= \frac{[\zeta\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}+\arcsin\zeta]_{\zeta_-}^{\zeta_+}}
{2(\zeta_+-\zeta_-)},
\end{align}$$ which are exactly the original node parameters $\tau_i=2\beta_i/3=2\Sigma_t p_i$ and $\kappa_i$ of Ref. [@psn2027] (Eq. (2.8d) for $\beta_i$; the generic polar set --- the source paper's three-polar protocol is the same structure with fixed quadrature nodes). These are P0 moments per polar sector --- the polar quadrature is not extended here; $I$ stays a partition parameter.

*Azimuthal projection: the $K$-mode segment space.* The essential new degree of freedom is the intra-segment angular representation. Within segment $m$, $\varphi\in[\varphi_-,\varphi_+]$ of width $\Delta\varphi$, centre $c_m$, half-width $\delta_m=\Delta\varphi/2$, set $\xi=(\varphi-c_m)/\delta_m\in[-1,1]$ and expand the even-parity angular flux in the orthonormal Legendre basis of the segment, $$\begin{equation}
e(\mathbf{r},\varphi) \simeq \sum_{a=0}^{A} b_a(\xi)\,e_a(\mathbf{r}),
\qquad b_a(\xi)=\sqrt{2a+1}\,P_a(\xi),
\label{eq:azexp}
\end{equation}$$ with $K=A{+}1$ unknowns per face per segment ($K{=}4$ at $A{=}3$) and $\langle f\rangle=\tfrac12\int_{-1}^{1}f\,d\xi$, so $\langle b_a b_b\rangle=\delta_{ab}$ and $\langle b_a\rangle=\delta_{a0}$. Projecting Eq. [\[eq:A1\]](#eq:A1){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:A1"}: multiply by each test basis function $b_a$, average over the segment and the polar sector; the basis is angle-space only, so the spatial derivatives come out of the angular integrals. The $K$ test equations per segment are the Galerkin projections of Eq. [\[eq:A1\]](#eq:A1){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:A1"} onto the segment basis; they supersede the original single segment-mean balance (Eq. (2.9a) of Ref. [@psn2027]) in the angular representation, without constituting a higher streaming-operator order of the original PSN construction. The collision term is diagonal by orthogonality, the isotropic source feeds only the $a{=}0$ mode, and the streaming term produces the three segment-averaged moment matrices $$\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}_m{}_{ab} = \big\langle b_a b_b\cos^2\varphi\big\rangle,
\qquad
\mathbf{S}_m{}_{ab} = \big\langle b_a b_b\sin^2\varphi\big\rangle,
\qquad
\mathbf{H}_m{}_{ab} = \big\langle b_a b_b\sin\varphi\cos\varphi\big\rangle,
\label{eq:CSH}
\end{equation}$$ evaluated once per segment by Gauss quadrature and cached. The matrices are symmetric, $\mathbf{C}+\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{I}_K$, $\mathbf{C}$ and $\mathbf{S}$ positive definite for any positive-width segment (grazing-adjacent segments can be ill-conditioned, which the spectral construction below monitors), and $\mathbf{H}$ is generically non-commuting with both.

*The directional diffusion coefficients.* Substituting the expansion [\[eq:azexp\]](#eq:azexp){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:azexp"} into the mother equation [\[eq:A1\]](#eq:A1){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:A1"} and expanding the directional operator inside a node of constant $\Sigma_t$: the in-plane projection $\boldsymbol{\Omega}_\perp=(\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\sin\theta\sin\varphi,0)$ is independent of $(x,y)$, so the streaming term acts purely on the spatial derivatives of the angular field, $$\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla\bigl(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla e\bigr)
=\sin^2\theta\Bigl(\cos^2\varphi\, e_{xx}
+2\sin\varphi\cos\varphi\, e_{xy}+\sin^2\varphi\, e_{yy}\Bigr),
\label{eq:stream}
\end{equation}$$ where $e_{xx}$, $e_{xy}$, $e_{yy}$ are the second spatial derivatives of $e$.

The projection then separates exactly. Multiply [\[eq:stream\]](#eq:stream){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:stream"} by the test basis $b_a$ and average over the segment $m$ and the polar sector $i$. The collision term is diagonal by orthogonality ($\langle b_a b_b\rangle=\delta_{ab}$), the isotropic source feeds only the $a{=}0$ mode, and the streaming term admits the expansion $e=\sum_b b_b\,e_b$ with the spatial derivatives passing through the angular average unchanged. Since $\sin^2\theta$ depends only on the polar angle, the $\theta$- and $\varphi$-averages factorize, and the contribution of the streaming term to the $a$-th test equation is $$\begin{equation}
-\frac{\tau_i}{\Sigma_t}\sum_{b=0}^{A}\Bigl(
C_{ab}\,e_{b,xx}+2H_{ab}\,e_{b,xy}+S_{ab}\,e_{b,yy}\Bigr),
\label{eq:proj}
\end{equation}$$ where $\tau_i=\langle\sin^2\theta\rangle_i$ is the sector's second polar moment and the angular integrals are exactly the moment matrices [\[eq:CSH\]](#eq:CSH){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:CSH"}: the three directional second derivatives $e_{xx}$, $e_{xy}$, $e_{yy}$ select $\mathbf{C}$, $\mathbf{H}$, $\mathbf{S}$ respectively, and no other combination enters. In vector form, with $\mathbf{e}_{xx}=(e_{0,xx},\ldots,e_{A,xx})^T$ and analogously for $e_{xy}$, $e_{yy}$, this is the projected body equation $$\begin{equation}
-\frac{\tau_i}{\Sigma_t}\Bigl(\mathbf{C}\,\mathbf{e}_{xx}
+2\mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{e}_{xy}+\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{e}_{yy}\Bigr)
+\Sigma_t\,\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{v}_0\,q(\mathbf{r}),
\label{eq:A7}
\end{equation}$$ where $\mathbf{e}=(e_0,\ldots,e_A)^T$, $\mathbf{v}_0=(1,0,\ldots,0)^T$, and $q$ is the five-term source (below). Identifying the *directional diffusion coefficients* $$\begin{equation}
\mathbf{D}_x = \frac{\tau_i}{\Sigma_t}\mathbf{C} = 2p\,\mathbf{C},
\qquad
\mathbf{D}_y = \frac{\tau_i}{\Sigma_t}\mathbf{S} = 2p\,\mathbf{S},
\qquad
\mathbf{D}_{xy} = \frac{\tau_i}{\Sigma_t}\mathbf{H} = 2p\,\mathbf{H},
\label{eq:A8}
\end{equation}$$ with $p=\beta_i/(3\Sigma_t)=\tau_i/(2\Sigma_t)$ the original node parameter (Eq. (2.8d) of Ref. [@psn2027] gives $\beta_i=\tfrac32\,\tau_i$).

**The $A{=}0$ limit reproduces the original face current exactly.** Restricting Eq. [\[eq:A8\]](#eq:A8){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:A8"} to the $a{=}0$ component and writing $c_m$ and $\Delta\varphi_m$ for the segment centre and width, the three scalar coefficients become $$\begin{equation}
(\mathbf{D}_x)_{00} = \frac{\tau_i}{2\Sigma_t}\,
\bigl(1+\gamma_m\cos 2c_m\bigr),\qquad
(\mathbf{D}_{xy})_{00} = \frac{\tau_i}{2\Sigma_t}\,
\gamma_m\sin 2c_m,\qquad
(\mathbf{D}_y)_{00} = \frac{\tau_i}{2\Sigma_t}\,
\bigl(1-\gamma_m\cos 2c_m\bigr),
\label{eq:CSH-A0}
\end{equation}$$ $\gamma_m=\sin\Delta\varphi_m/\Delta\varphi_m$ as in Eq. (2.8b) of Ref. [@psn2027]. With the face-current relation (Section [\[sec:face\]](#sec:face){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:face"} below), $J_n=-(n_x\mathbf{D}_x+n_y\mathbf{D}_{xy})\bar{e}_x^{\,n}
-(n_x\mathbf{D}_{xy}+n_y\mathbf{D}_y)\bar{e}_y^{\,n}$, the $a{=}0$ component is then $J_n=-\hat{\mathbf{n}}^T D^{(0)}_{im}
\overline{\nabla\phi}^{\,n}$ with $D^{(0)}_{im}=\frac{\tau_i}{2\Sigma_t}
\bigl[(1-\gamma_m)\mathbf{I}+2\gamma_m\mathbf{d}_m\mathbf{d}_m^T\bigr]$ ($\mathbf{d}_m=(\cos c_m,\sin c_m)^T$) the original face-current tensor, *for any face normal and any segment* --- the directional face current at $A{=}0$ is the original Eq. (2.8b), not an approximation to it.

**The original wave number.** The scalar $k^2=3\Sigma_t^2$ of Eq. [\[eq:diff3c\]](#eq:diff3c){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:diff3c"} is the $A{=}0$ special case of Eq. [\[eq:A8\]](#eq:A8){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:A8"} in which both averages are taken over their full ranges --- the azimuthal segment over the complete half plane ($\Delta\varphi_m=\pi$, $\gamma_m=0$, $(\mathbf{C})_{00}=(\mathbf{S})_{00}=\tfrac12$, $\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{0}$) *and* the polar range over the full solid angle, $\tau_i=\tfrac23$. Then $\mathbf{D}=\frac{\tau_i}{2\Sigma_t}\mathbf{I}=\frac{1}{3\Sigma_t}\mathbf{I}$ and the single decay constant $k=\sqrt{\Sigma_t/\tfrac{1}{3\Sigma_t}}=\sqrt{3}\,\Sigma_t$ is recovered. For a truncated polar range the decay rates are $\sqrt{\Sigma_t/\lambda_j}$ with $\lambda_j$ the spectrum of $(\tau_i/\Sigma_t)\mathbf{C}$: at $A{=}0$ over the full half-plane that spectrum is the single value $\tau_i/(2\Sigma_t)$, so the two scales coincide only at $\tau_i=\tfrac23$ --- a full-half-plane $A{=}0$ statement; for a finite segment the whole $\mathbf{C}$ spectrum, not the polar moment alone, sets the rates. The angular-moment closure generalizes that one decay constant to the spectrum of the segment-resolved matrix; at $A{=}0$ the two discretizations still differ, because the original *body* uses a single scalar wave number (full-solid-angle for the generic set, per-sector for the three-polar protocol) while the directional body uses the *segment-resolved* decay rates (Section [2.3.1.1](#sec:a3a0){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:a3a0"} below).

*Rectangular nodes: the per-node widths enter only through the spatial scales.* The angular construction above is width-independent: neither the projected equation [\[eq:A7\]](#eq:A7){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:A7"} nor the moment matrices [\[eq:CSH\]](#eq:CSH){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:CSH"} contains a node width, and the same is true of the original method, whose spatial shape (Eq. (2.11a) of Ref. [@psn2027]) uses the scalar wave number $k=\sqrt{3}\,\Sigma_t$ for the generic polar set and $k_i=\sqrt{2}\,\Sigma_t/\mu_i$ for the three-polar protocol, with the node widths entering only through the spatial coordinates and the hyperbolic arguments. For rectangular nodes the source basis is rescaled by the per-axis coordinates $\eta_x=2x/h_x$, $\eta_y=2y/h_y$ (the same five spatial moments), and the hyperbolic family below carries per-axis arguments $U_x=h_xK_x/2$, $U_y=h_yK_y/2$, so each angular mode decays across the node at its own rate $k_j=\sqrt{\Sigma_t/\lambda_j}$ over the actual width of each axis. Nodes meet corner-to-corner only (no T-junctions), so on this conforming grid every face has a single shared tangential length and the face-current matching is unchanged (the two nodes on either side of a face need not have the same width in the normal direction). When all widths are equal the rectangular operators reduce exactly to the square-node path, and the two are verified node-by-node to machine precision (the regression of Section 2.2).

*The body function: hyperbolic functions of the matrix spectrum.* With the five-term spatial source $$\begin{equation}
q(x,y) = q_0+q_{1x}P_1(\eta_x)+q_{1y}P_1(\eta_y)+q_{2x}P_2(\eta_x)
+q_{2y}P_2(\eta_y),\qquad
\eta_x=\frac{2x}{h_x},\quad \eta_y=\frac{2y}{h_y},
\label{eq:source5}
\end{equation}$$ (the same five spatial source moments as the original method; the spatial function family is unchanged, written in the rescaled per-axis coordinates), the even-parity body is sought in the separated hyperbolic family $$\begin{equation}
\mathbf{e}(x,y) = F_x(x)\mathbf{a}_x+G_x(x)\mathbf{b}_x
+F_y(y)\mathbf{a}_y+G_y(y)\mathbf{b}_y+\mathbf{c}
+\mathbf{v}_0\,q(x,y)/\Sigma_t,
\label{eq:A10}
\end{equation}$$ $F_x(x)=\cosh(K_x x)\cosh(U_x)^{-1}$, $G_x(x)=\sinh(K_x x)\cosh(U_x)^{-1}$ (and y-analogues), with the positive-definite square roots $K_x=(\Sigma_t\mathbf{D}_x^{-1})^{1/2}$, $U_x=h_xK_x/2$ and similarly for $y$. Each scalar mode of the eigen-decomposition of $\mathbf{D}_x$ contributes a hyperbolic pair at its own decay constant $k_j=\sqrt{\Sigma_t/\lambda_j}$; the scaled-hyperbolic form $\cosh v/\cosh u$ (all exponents non-positive) guards the grazing directions against overflow. The quadratic source fixes the constant vector by $\partial_x^2 P_2(\eta_x)=12/h_x^2$: $$\begin{equation}
\mathbf{c} = \frac{12}{\Sigma_t^2}\Big(
\mathbf{D}_x\mathbf{v}_0\,\frac{q_{2x}}{h_x^2}
+\mathbf{D}_y\mathbf{v}_0\,\frac{q_{2y}}{h_y^2}\Big).
\label{eq:A11}
\end{equation}$$ The family has $e_{xy}=0$; it satisfies the *projected* node equation Eq. [\[eq:A7\]](#eq:A7){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:A7"} inside this separated space --- the general mixed-derivative response is not recovered, and $\mathbf{D}_{xy}$ acts through the face tangential-gradient current (below), not through the body. All matrix functions of one axis ($\tanh U$, $\tanh U/U$, and the space-integrals $I_1,I_2$ defined below) are built from the *same* eigen-decomposition; in particular $\mathbf{D}_{xy}\tanh U_y$ is not commutable and keeps that order.

*Face currents: the $4K\times4K$ local system.*[]{#sec:face label="sec:face"} With $t_x=\tanh U_x$ and the face operators $A_x=\mathbf{D}_xK_xt_x$, $B_x=\mathbf{D}_xK_x$, $H_y=\frac{2}{h_y}\mathbf{D}_{xy}\tanh U_y$, $H_x=\frac{2}{h_x}\mathbf{D}_{xy}\tanh U_x$ (y-analogues of $A_x,B_x$), the outward face-current coefficients of the four faces satisfy the block relation $$\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{J}_{x+}\\\mathbf{J}_{x-}\\
\mathbf{J}_{y+}\\\mathbf{J}_{y-}\end{pmatrix}}_{\mathbf{J}}
= \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}
-A_x&-B_x&0&-H_y\\
-A_x& B_x&0& H_y\\
0&-H_x&-A_y&-B_y\\
0& H_x&-A_y& B_y
\end{pmatrix}}_{\mathcal{K}}
\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{a}_x\\\mathbf{b}_x\\\mathbf{a}_y\\\mathbf{b}_y\end{pmatrix}
+ \mathcal{O}\,\mathbf{q},
\label{eq:A13}
\end{equation}$$ which is the tensor face-current relation $\mathbf{J}_n = -\big(n_x\mathbf{D}_x+n_y\mathbf{D}_{xy}\big)\overline{\mathbf{e}}_x^{\,n}
-\big(n_x\mathbf{D}_{xy}+n_y\mathbf{D}_y\big)\overline{\mathbf{e}}_y^{\,n}$: *every angular mode sees its own directional diffusion coefficient*, which is the origin of the name. The source-offset block $\mathcal{O}$ is the face evaluation of the five-term particular solution, with an overall $1/\Sigma_t$ factor: its rows are the columns $\mathbf{D}_x\mathbf{v}_0$, $\mathbf{D}_y\mathbf{v}_0$, $\mathbf{D}_{xy}\mathbf{v}_0$ scaled by $(1/\Sigma_t)(2/h)$ and $(1/\Sigma_t)(6/h)$, so *high angular modes do receive source contribution* even though the source itself is purely $a{=}0$. Inverting Eq. [\[eq:A13\]](#eq:A13){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:A13"} (by direct solve, not explicit inversion) yields the local response mapping $\mathbf{q}$ and $\mathbf{J}\mapsto$ face means and the five spatial moments. At $K{=}4$ the local coefficient system is $16\times16$ per node (plus the five source columns); the angular quadrature points used to build Eq. [\[eq:CSH\]](#eq:CSH){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:CSH"} are construction points, not transport unknowns. The five spatial moments returned to the source update are $$\begin{align}
m_0 &= \mathbf{v}_0^T\overline{\mathbf{e}}, \qquad
m_{1x} = 3\,\mathbf{v}_0^T I_1(U_x)\,\mathbf{b}_x + q_{1x}/\Sigma_t,\\
m_{2x} &= 5\,\mathbf{v}_0^T I_2(U_x)\,\mathbf{a}_x + q_{2x}/\Sigma_t,
\end{align}$$ with $I_1(U)=U^{-1}(I-U^{-1}\tanh U)$, $I_2(U)=U^{-1}(\tanh U-3I_1(U))$ and y-analogues. The angular-zeroth extraction is performed *after* the full matrix--vector product (e.g. $(3\,I_1\mathbf{b}_x)_0$, not $3(I_1)_{00}(\mathbf{b}_x)_0$): high angular modes feed the scalar source through the off-diagonal entries, which is exactly the coupling that the single-mode closure cannot represent. The node volume balance $\overline{\mathbf{e}}=\mathbf{v}_0 q_0/\Sigma_t
-(\mathbf{J}_{x+}+\mathbf{J}_{x-})/(h_x\Sigma_t)
-(\mathbf{J}_{y+}+\mathbf{J}_{y-})/(h_y\Sigma_t)$ holds for every angular mode simultaneously.

*Assembly: face fluxes, boundaries, and the eigenproblem.* The signed face flux entering the global connection is the original PSN convention with the basis promoted to a matrix: $\Phi_n = \frac{\kappa_i}{2}\mathbf{E}_n\,\mathbf{L}_n$, $\big(\mathbf{E}_n\big)_{ab}=\langle b_a b_b\cos(\varphi-\varphi_n)\rangle$, with $\mathbf{L}_n$ the face-mean even-parity vector, $\big(\mathbf{L}_n\big)_a = \frac{1}{|\partial n|}\int_{\partial n}
e_a\,ds$; at $A{=}0$ this is exactly the original $\alpha\,\times$ face-mean. The internal face closure uses the same raw face-mean continuity and anti-symmetric outward-current pairing as the original method; on a uniform even $M$ grid with axis-aligned nodes every segment has a single sign of $\Omega\cdot n$, so the connection is exact in this $L$-representation. Mirror reflection maps segment $m$ to $M{-}1{-}m$ with local $\xi\mapsto-\xi$, which in the $K$-mode representation is $L_m = R\,L_{m'}$, $J_m = -R\,J_{m'}$ with $R=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,1,-1,\ldots)$ --- odd modes reverse their reflection sign; the moment matrices obey the corresponding identities $C_{m'}=RC_mR$, $H_{m'}=-RH_mR$. Vacuum is the hemispheric current $J = B\,L$ with $B$ the positive-definite matrix $\bigl(B\bigr)_{ab}=\kappa_i\langle b_a b_b
|\cos(\varphi-\varphi_n)|\rangle$ (the same normal-relative form on every face: $\varphi_n=0$ on $x$ faces, $\pi/2$ on $y$ faces), $\kappa_i$ the first polar moment (not $\sqrt{\tau_i}$); at $A{=}0$ this is $B_{00}=2|\alpha_{imn}|$, the original vacuum condition. Multi-group assembly keeps the original structure: each group's five-moment response $R_g$ is coupled through the isotropic scattering and fission blocks, the discrete eigenproblem is non-symmetric and is driven by the same outer fission-source (power) iteration as the original implementation; each outer sweep solves the node-coupling system with a Krylov method (GMRES). The outer iteration stops when the relative eigenvalue change $|\Delta k|/k$ and the fission-source iterate change both fall below $10^{-7}$: the iterate change is the $\ell_2$ norm of the difference between successive fission sources divided by the larger of the previous source's norm and unity, $r_F=\|s^{(n+1)}-s^{(n)}\|_2/\max(\|s^{(n)}\|_2,1)$, a scaled iterate change --- relative when the source norm exceeds unity, absolute below it --- with both iterates produced on the same normalisation convention; the GMRES inner solve runs to a relative linear-residual tolerance of $10^{-7}$ (absolute tolerance zero) --- the same value as the outer gate, as used by the production sweep driver; the library default of $10^{-11}$ applies to standalone CLI runs --- with a restart length of $20$ inner iterations, at most $800$ restart cycles per linear system (the $\mathtt{maxiter}$ interface parameter of the scipy implementation as invoked). This linear-system tolerance is separate from the outer stopping criteria.

#### Relation between legacy PSN and the directional $A{=}0$ member {#sec:a3a0}

At $A{=}0$ the two branches of the implementation share the *face-current constitutive law* --- the $(0,0)$ entries of Eq. [\[eq:CSH\]](#eq:CSH){reference-type="eqref" reference="eq:CSH"} are the original segment means $\langle\cos^2\varphi\rangle_m$ etc. --- and the interface flux/current connection. The *body* differs. The original branch keeps the published scalar wave number, $k=\sqrt{3}\,\Sigma_t$ for the generic polar set and $k_i=\sqrt{2}\,\Sigma_t/\mu_i$ ($\mu_i=\sin\theta_i$) for the three-polar protocol, while the directional branch evaluates its hyperbolic functions at the segment-resolved spectrum of $\mathbf{D}_x,\mathbf{D}_y$, i.e. at $\sqrt{\Sigma_t/\lambda_j}$ with $\lambda_j$ the segment-dependent eigenvalues. A shared constitutive law and connection do not make the condensed nodal response $J,q\mapsto L,m$ identical --- the body wave number enters the face-current block matrices directly --- so the two discretizations agree on the face current at equal gradients but not inside the node: this is precisely the body/face scale split of the original method (Section above) carried into the new branch, and it is why the directional $A{=}0$ solution is a distinct, segment-resolved discretization rather than a reproduction of the legacy state matrix (verified numerically over all 2016 node-response configurations of a C5G7 pin cell: 3 polar points $\times$ 7 groups $\times$ 24 azimuthal segments $\times$ 4 material groups: the original branch at $A{=}0$ reproduces the legacy node response to $4.8\times10^{-13}$, while the directional branch at $A{=}0$ differs by $\mathcal{O}(1)$ in the body scales, with a decay-rate spread of $0.62$--$45.9$ across the half-plane segments). The $A{=}0$ directional solution is the correct limiting member of the new family; the $A{=}1,2,3$ results below are reported with it as the reference closure.

# Implementation verification and reproduction

## Local algebra and the $A{=}0$ regression

#### Verification of the local construction.

Before any benchmark was re-run, the local node response was verified against independently constructed solutions: for $A{=}0\ldots3$, five azimuthal segment widths, both aspect ratios of a $2$-cm node, and all five unit sources (plus one mixed source), a spatial field is built from arbitrary homogeneous coefficients and source, the four face flux/current vectors and the five spatial moments are computed by independent numerical integration, and compared against the local response matrix. Maximum normalized differences over 240 cases: face response and five moments $4.5\times10^{-12}$, constant-vector recovery from the volume balance $8.5\times10^{-12}$, strong residual of the projected body equation $1.0\times10^{-15}$. These verify the discretization *as written*; they are not a transport-accuracy statement.

These checks concern the implementation of the specified local responses and discrete systems. Agreement between two algebraic solution paths supports implementation consistency for those systems; it does not remove their angular or spatial approximation errors. Likewise, the original-scale regression establishes recovery of the legacy implementation under that setting, rather than equivalence of the directional $A{=}0$ closure and original PSN.

# Numerical assessment

## Configuration

C5G7 is the OECD Nuclear Energy Agency (NEA) mixed-oxide (MOX) benchmark [@nea2003; @smith2004], the standard test for uranium-oxide (UOX)/MOX inter-cell transport: 17$\times$`<!-- -->`{=html}17-pin fuel assemblies on a 1.26 cm pin pitch (UO$_2$ plus three MOX enrichments), guide tubes and fission chambers, in a 7-group structure. (The C5G7 family was later extended to time-dependent calculations [@c5g7td]; we solve the steady 2-D problem.) We solve the **C5G7-2D 1/4-core** problem: a 2$\times$`<!-- -->`{=html}2 block of fuel assemblies (UO2 on the diagonal, MOX on the anti-diagonal) plus an L-shaped one-assembly water reflector, 51$\times$`<!-- -->`{=html}51 pins, 64.26 cm on a side. Following Ref. [@nea2003] we apply *vacuum* boundaries on the two open (right, top) edges and *reflective* boundaries on the two symmetry (left, bottom) edges. We model the pin cell in three ways. *(i)* The standard pin-homogenised nodal model folds each fuel pin cell into a single homogenised node (fuel$+$water; pure water for reflector and guide pins). *(ii)* The rectangular-node model of Section 2.2 represents each pin by multiple homogeneous material rectangles: a $3\times3$ base tiling per pin (one fuel square plus eight water strips), so the explicit square-fuel model resolves the fuel--moderator boundary across nodes rather than assigning both materials to a single node; the equal-area replacement of the circular fuel by a square remains a geometry approximation. *(iii)* The local angular-moment campaign uses a $4\times4$ tiling obtained by splitting the fuel region into four rectangles ($2\times2$ fuel sub-nodes). The original rectangular PSN campaign and the angular-moment campaign thus use the same square-fuel boundary but different numerical meshes. The 7-group cross sections are the published C5G7 multi-group set in all cases.

Eigenvalue differences in the present tables are reported as $\Delta k=10^5(k-k_{\mathrm{ref}})$, following the convention used for the reported numerical data; this quantity is distinct from the relative eigenvalue change used to stop an iteration. References for the pin-homogenised model and the explicit square-fuel model are kept separate, and comparisons with the circular-fuel literature reference additionally contain a difference between geometry models.

Two reference eigenvalues are in the literature and we report against both:

- $\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{MCNP}}=1.18655$ ($\pm0.008\%$), the 2-D multi-group MCNP value in the original NEA report [@nea2003; @smith2004];

- $\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}=1.18646$ (1.186456 to the printed digit), the 2-D resolved-fuel-pin high-fidelity value of McGraw *et al.* [@mcgraw2014], quoted as the reference in the INL Rattlesnake convergence study [@rattlesnake], which explicitly notes that the benchmark-report value 1.18655 is $\sim$`<!-- -->`{=html}10 pcm high and converges to $\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}=1.186446$ ($\sim$`<!-- -->`{=html}1 pcm from the McGraw reference).

We report all deviations against 1.18646 (the more accurate high-fidelity value). The original NEA report tabulated relative errors against its MCNP reference 1.18655; re-referenced to 1.18646, those values shift by $\sim$`<!-- -->`{=html}9 pcm.

## Study I --- the pinwise-homogenised nodal model

The standard nodal treatment folds each pin's fuel and moderator into a single homogenised node. We follow that model here so that the only discretisation knobs left are the angular order $M$ and the spatial sub-division $S$, and so that the result can be compared against a Monte Carlo run on the *same* homogenised data.

#### Pinwise homogenisation.

The homogenised 7-group cross sections are built pin by pin with the **flux-weighted self-shielding** recipe: an infinite-medium pin cell ($r=0.54$ cm, 1.26 cm pitch) is solved with an explicit fuel/water MOC calculation, and the fuel-group cross sections are weighted by the self-shielded infinite-medium flux (scattering by the source-group flux). Guide tubes are area-weighted. This is the same self-shielding-aware homogenisation that nodal practice uses in production; it removes most of the resonance self-shielding error that a plain area-weighted average leaves behind.

#### Monte Carlo reference, computed directly.

Rather than lean on a published reference for the homogenised model, we compute the reference ourselves with OpenMC [@openmc2015] on the exact same pinwise-homogenised cross sections, so that the two sides differ only in the transport method. Each calculation uses 20000 active batches of 100000 particles (collision estimator); the eigenvalue is the mean over the last 20000 batches and its standard error is $\sigma_{\mathrm{batch}}/\sqrt{N}$, which we audit to be $\approx$`<!-- -->`{=html}3.4--3.5 pcm. We run the single UO$_2$ assembly, the single MOX assembly and the 1/4 core, each with *homogenised* (pin-node) and, where needed, *explicit* (resolved fuel/water) geometry. The three *homogenised* runs are the reference for the pinwise-homogenised PSN sweep below:

- single UO$_2$ assembly: $1.3336285$ ($\pm3.5$ pcm),

- single MOX assembly: $1.1853993$ ($\pm3.3$ pcm),

- 1/4 core: $1.1868230$ ($\pm3.4$ pcm).

We sweep $S\in\{1,\dots,6\}$ and $M\in\{2,4,8,12,16,24\}$ on all three geometries (nodes $(17S)^2$ for both assemblies, $(51S)^2$ for the core), with the pinwise-homogenised C5G7 cross sections, and converge every point to $10^{-10}$ in the relative eigenvalue change --- all 108 points of Table [2](#tab:bww){reference-type="ref" reference="tab:bww"}. ## Study II --- the rectangular-node model with explicit fuel/moderator rectangles

#### Motivation and layout.

Study I isolates the discretisation error of the nodal PSN model, but its eigenvalue is still anchored to whatever homogenisation model feeds it. To remove that model from the problem we use the rectangular-node extension of Section 2.2: the circular C5G7 fuel rod ($r=0.54$ cm) is replaced by an equal-area fuel square of side $s=\sqrt{\pi r^{2}}=0.95713$ cm centred on the same 1.26 cm pitch, so that every pin cell becomes a $3\times3$ array of rectangles (one fuel square plus eight water strips) and the whole core is a globally edge-aligned rectangular tiling. Figure [3](#fig:layout){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:layout"} shows the layout. The pin pitch, the rod positions (including Gd pins, which are the centre rectangle of their $3\times3$ block) and the total fuel area are unchanged; the layout was verified node by node --- all 2601 pin-cell centre rectangles of the 1/4 core coincide with the 2601 pin cells of the homogenised grid, and the total area reproduces $4129.35~\mathrm{cm}^2$ exactly. Un-homogenised 7-group cross sections (fuel, water, Gd, MOX) are then assigned directly to the rectangles. Two discretisation knobs are now open: the angular order $M$ and the uniform spatial subdivision $S$ (Section 2.2), and we sweep both.

The sweep shows three things. (i) On the UO$_2$ assembly the best point ($S{=}1,M{=}24$, $+1.2$ pcm) lies within the OpenMC standard error ($\pm3.6$ pcm): with a low-absorption fuel this tiling reaches the reported reference accuracy on this geometry, and every deeper subdivision is worse, monotonically from $-28.1$ pcm at $S{=}2$ to $-56.5$ pcm at $S{=}6$ --- within this tiling and the tested range, subdivision alone moves the result away from the reference. (ii) On the MOX assembly the tiling as constructed ($S{=}1$) is badly off even at $M{=}24$ ($-98.9$ pcm); a single subdivision to $S{=}2$ improves it to its best point ($-33.5$ pcm), and any further subdivision ($S{=}3\ldots6$) degrades it again, back to $-128.4$ pcm --- the non-monotonic $S$-response with a single interior optimum at $S{=}2$ is the clearest signature of the gap-node problem below. #### The slender water gap.

The $S$-saturation pattern above points to a geometric cause. In the $3\times3$ tiling the water-gap nodes are intrinsically slender: each edge strip node measures $s \times w = 0.9571\times0.1514$ cm, an aspect ratio of $6.3{:}1$ (the corner nodes, $w\times w$, are square but carry little physics). The uniform $S$ subdivision divides *both* dimensions by $S$, so every sub-node inherits the parent's aspect ratio --- $S$-subdivision shrinks the strip size and optical thickness but never reshapes the strips. The residual eigenvalue bias of Table [3](#tab:rectsm){reference-type="ref" reference="tab:rectsm"} is therefore plausibly anchored in the strip nodes; within the tested range, $S$-subdivision alone does not remove the deviation from the reference, which is consistent with the UO$_2$ rows that degrade smoothly and monotonically as $S$ grows at fixed $M$ (weak water coupling: subdivision adds nodes but never re-shapes the strips), the MOX rows whose only relief is a single subdivision --- best $S{=}2$, degrading again for $S{\ge}3$ (strong fuel--water coupling in the gap) --- and the 1/4 core, whose $S{=}2$ row is uniformly worse than $S{=}1$ across all $M$.

#### Performance at $M{=}24$, $S{=}3$ on the rectangular core.

This configuration has $459\times459=210{,}681$ nodes and the same material geometry and pure seven-group cross sections as the rectangular OpenMC reference. OpenPSN uses a diffusion-preconditioned Ritz projection and a shared sparse Cholesky preconditioner. The non-diagonal Cholesky entries use column-scaled 16-bit integers and variable-length row deltas. The diagonal, matrix-vector products and residual checks use double precision. Each angular sweep assembles and reduces one azimuth at a time.

At a budget of 60 parallel units, the median fine-grid transport sweep time for sweeps 2–11 decreased from 160.16 s with the earlier hybrid incomplete-Cholesky/PCG implementation to 19.17 s, corresponding to a speedup of **$8.35\times$ per sweep**. The hybrid run reached the 45-minute time limit after 11 sweeps. The standard CLI run completed 22 sweeps including one independent verification sweep in 1261.48 s end to end. Factor and operator setup took 691.31 s. Peak host memory was 28.83 GiB and the compressed factors and double-precision operator pool occupied 9.419 GiB.

The converged eigenvalue is $k_\mathrm{eff}=1.186767156487$. The same-geometry OpenMC reference is $1.187217554586$ (one standard error of 3.41 pcm), giving a signed difference of $-45.04$ pcm under the convention $\Delta k=10^{5}(k_\mathrm{eff}-k_\mathrm{ref})$. The independently recomputed fine-grid relative source residual is $2.57\times10^{-12}$. The reported relative eigenvalue residual is $9.43\times10^{-12}$, the maximum of the last Ritz eigenvalue change and the verification difference. The example directory includes the timed sweep series and the configuration used for this comparison.

## Study III --- the segmentwise angular-moment variant at $A{=}3$

The reproduced PSN calculations provide the baseline for the assessment. Low eigenvalue errors and accurate normalised spatial distributions at selected parameters remain substantive results of the original formulation. The rectangular-node results also show sensitivity to the tested spatial and angular settings. These observations motivate evaluation of an alternative angular representation, but do not identify a unique error mechanism. The following results therefore assess the separate local angular-moment branch (Section [2.3](#sec:amvariant){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:amvariant"}), rather than treating it as an unchanged PSN operator with additional coefficients.

#### The angular-resolution difficulty of Study II.

The results of the preceding paragraphs show that the rectangular-node model still carries a large eigenvalue bias on the MOX assembly even at $M{=}24$ ($-98.9$ pcm at $S{=}1$, $-33.5$ pcm at its best $S{=}2$), and that on every geometry the $M$-convergence is non-monotonic at low order, flattening only at the highest segment count. The $S$-response is non-monotonic as well: on the MOX row a single subdivision buys the best point and any further subdivision degrades it again. The tested refinements do not establish convergence to the reference for these configurations. The legacy PSN reconstruction already contains continuous directional dependence through its gradient-generated odd part; it does not retain independent higher even-parity modes within each segment. The variant assessed here (Section [2.3](#sec:amvariant){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:amvariant"}) introduces those modes and changes the body closure. We classify it as a multi-$P_N$-type segmentwise moment method with a PSN-inspired nodal response, rather than as the original PSN closure at a higher order. Here $A{=}3$ denotes a cubic polynomial in local azimuth, not global spherical-harmonic $P_3$ or SP$_3$; the polar treatment is unchanged.

#### The checkerboard of Ref. [@psn2027] at $A{=}3$.

The first re-computation of Study II's protocol is the strong/weak absorber checkerboard of Figure 3 of Ref. [@psn2027]: a $2\times2$ base cell of $1$ cm nodes (all edges reflective), one group, fuel $\Sigma_t{=}1.5$, $\Sigma_s{=}1.35$, $\nu\Sigma_f{=}0.24$ and absorber $\Sigma_t{=}1.0$ with $\Sigma_s{=}0.93$ (weak) or $0.30$ (strong). We uniformly subdivide to $2S\times2S$ nodes and sweep $S{=}1\ldots20$ against $M{=}2,4,8,12,16,24$ (120 points per absorber, 240 in total) with the $A{=}3$ closure and a $10^{-7}$ convergence gate. The polar partition is the generic even set with $I{=}30$ segments (15 independent sectors); the source paper's three-polar protocol was swept as well ($S{=}1\ldots12$, 72 points per absorber). References: the OpenMC values of the source paper, $1.12974$ (weak) and $0.51673$ (strong), quoted to five decimals ($\approx\pm0.5$ pcm precision limit).

Figure [7](#fig:cbA3){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:cbA3"} gives the $I{=}30$ sweep. Four readings. (i) The coarse-grid part converges as second order in the node size: $S{=}1\to6$ reduces $|\Delta k|$ by a factor $\sim$`<!-- -->`{=html}28 at $M{=}24$ on both absorbers (weak $+458.6\to+16.1$, strong $+2430.4\to+89.5$ pcm), and the $M$-columns flatten from $M{=}8$ on --- the angular-segment count is not the bottleneck once the segment count is above 8. (ii) The best point of the whole sweep is the weak absorber at $S{=}12$, $M{=}16/24$: $-0.1$ pcm, i.e. at the precision limit of the five-decimal reference; on the strong absorber the best point is $S{=}14$, $M{=}24$ ($-0.5$ pcm) / $S{=}13$, $M{=}8$ ($-1.9$ pcm). (iii) Beyond $S{\approx}12$ the deviation from the reference stops decreasing, and the fine-grid rows settle near a small residual within this scan: over $S{=}10\ldots20$ each $M{=}24$ column is fit by $b+c_2/S^2$ to $0.02$ pcm RMS (weak) and $0.08$ pcm RMS (strong), whereas the one-term $b+c_1/S$ form fits the same data at $0.16$/$0.84$ pcm RMS (the fit uses the unrounded sweep values) --- roughly a factor of $9$ (weak) to $10$ (strong) worse, i.e. the decay is second-order-like rather than linear. The finite-range data are thus consistent with a spatial contribution decaying as $S^{-2}$ plus a residual that persists at the fixed angular discretization --- a reading of a finite scan, not a proven asymptotic error decomposition; the polar-set comparison below ($I{=}60$ moves the $S{=}20$ point by $+2.9$ pcm weak, $+15.3$ pcm strong) shows that residual responds to the polar partition, but does not by itself establish $I{=}60$ as converged. Within this scan the points closest to the reference sit at $S{=}12$--$14$; beyond that range a larger $S$ adds cost without further reducing the deviation from the reference. (iv) The $M{=}2$ column's negative bias deepens with $S$ (weak $-51.8$, strong $-259.5$ at $S{=}20$): the two-segment azimuthal representation carries a structural streaming error that refinement exposes rather than hides.

Two effects are visible in the fine-grid rows and worth isolating. First, the signed-error crossing of zero between the coarse-grid positive bias and the fine-grid offset (e.g. weak $S{=}11$--$14$ at $M{=}16/24$) is consistent with cancellation between a decaying spatial contribution and a finite-angular-discretization offset: repeating the $I{=}30$ point at $S{=}20$, $M{=}24$, $A{=}3$ with the even polar set doubled to $I{=}60$ moves the weak-absorber value from $-3.8$ to $-0.9$ pcm and the strong-absorber value from $-11.6$ to $+3.7$ pcm. That comparison demonstrates polar-sector sensitivity; it does not establish a loss of second-order spatial convergence. The two polar sets do not by themselves prove the $I{=}60$ residual is converged, nor do they, on the same finite-range data, separate a polar-quadrature offset from any residual spatial-basis contribution --- they show the fine-grid residual responds to the polar partition, and no more. Second, the polar partition also explains the coarse/fine gap between the two protocols: on the source paper's three-polar protocol the same fine-grid points sit at $+3.9$ (weak) and $+28.6$ (strong) pcm at $S{=}12$, $M{=}24$, versus $-0.1$ and $+7.1$ pcm with $I{=}30$ --- the extra polar sectors pay off only on the finer meshes, where the sensitivity to the polar discretisation is more visible than the spatial contribution; at $S{=}1$ the two polar sets agree to within a few pcm.

#### The $2\times2$ fuel-split tiling.

The $A{=}3$ closure is run on the C5G7 pin cell with the fuel square split $2\times2$: each pin cell becomes a $4\times4$ block of rectangles --- four fuel sub-nodes of side $s/2{=}0.4786$ cm and a water ring of corner blocks ($0.1514^2$ cm$^2$) and edge strips ($0.4786\times0.1514$ cm, aspect $3.2{:}1$) --- Figure [8](#fig:rect4layout){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:rect4layout"}. The $17\times17$ assembly is $68\times68{=}4624$ rectangles and the 1/4 core $204\times204{=}41616$; pin positions, fuel area and total area are unchanged. The $2\times2$ split is the tiling in which the edge water strips --- the channels that carry streaming across the pin --- carry a tangential extent of $s/2$ (aspect $3.2{:}1$), halved from $s$ (aspect $6.3{:}1$) in the $3\times3$ tiling, while remaining dedicated water nodes on the fuel/water interface as they already were. It is the tiling the $A{=}3$ closure is run on: the angular correction and the intra-node spatial split act on different parts of the error, and the $S$ sub-division of Section 2.2 remains available on top of it.

#### C5G7 at $A{=}3$: assemblies and core.

Figure [9](#fig:a3mconv){reference-type="ref" reference="fig:a3mconv"} gives the $A{=}3$ sweep on the two $17\times17$ assemblies, $S{=}1,2$ against $M{=}2,4,8,12,16,24$ (24 points, every point converged to $10^{-7}$), and the $S{=}1$ row of the 1/4-core sweep on the same tiling, against the same-geometry pure-XS OpenMC references ($1.3347260$ UO$_2$, $1.1851053$ MOX, $1.1872176$ core; each $\approx\pm3.3$--$3.6$ pcm). The contrast with the old closure of Study II is direct, but with a caveat: the old closure ran on the $3\times3$ tiling, where the MOX assembly sat at $-98.9$ pcm ($S{=}1$, $M{=}24$) and $-33.5$ pcm at its best, while the $A{=}3$ closure runs on the $2\times2$ fuel-split tiling, where it sits at $-0.9$ pcm at $M{=}12$, $S{=}1$, inside the Monte Carlo standard error; on the $S{=}2$ row the same tiling sits at $-9.8$ pcm at $M{=}24$. The two tilings are not the same mesh ($4624$ vs $2601$ rectangles per assembly at $S{=}1$), so this contrast measures the new setting --- finer fuel split together with the $A{=}3$ angular closure --- as a whole rather than the angular order in isolation. ## Study IV --- the finite-sector diffusion closure

Study III added even-parity angular modes inside each angular segment and changed the body closure. This section assesses the branch that keeps the PSN idea in its literal form: the transport equation is reduced, per finite angular cell, to a diffusion-type equation whose coefficients are the exact second-moment tensor of the directions in that cell, and the resulting systems are assembled over space exactly as a diffusion calculation would be. The branch is implemented as the closures `sector_diffusion` (one field per Gauss direction of a sector) and `sector_average` (one field per finite angular cell, plus local azimuthal modes $A{=}0,\ldots,4$). Both are selected from the input file (`angular.model: generic`, `angular.closure: sector_average`, `angular.azimuth_degree`, `angular.polar_degree`, `angular.slope_space`); the driver `scripts/sector_diffusion_benchmark.py` reproduces every point below, and `scripts/study4_core_report.py` rebuilds the core tables from the recorded run logs. The same closure is written up on its own terms in `paper/sector_method.tex` --- even-parity reduction, sector tensors, the counterexamples that bound the word "exact", the test-problem inventory and the cost records --- which compiles and reads independently of this paper and shares its generated tables.

#### Formulation.

Inside a material-constant node, decomposing the angular flux into its even and odd parts under $\boldsymbol{\Omega}\rightarrow-\boldsymbol{\Omega}$ gives, for isotropic scattering and fission, the exact relation $\psi^{-}=-(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla u)/\Sigma_t$ for the even-parity flux $u$. Eliminating $\psi^{-}$ yields, for each retained direction,

$$-\nabla\cdot\!\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{\mathsf T}}{\Sigma_t}\nabla u\right) +\Sigma_t u = q ,$$

with the vacuum boundary condition carrying $|\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{n}|u$ and specularly reflected directions sharing the trace value on the reflecting face. That equation is discretised in space by continuous biquadratic Lagrange nodes, with the body diffusion term, the normal current across material interfaces and the vacuum term taken from one weak form, so no separate face-current model is introduced. Scattering and fission are projected onto nine orthogonal spatial moments per material node. Azimuth is split into $M$ sectors per half-plane, each carrying $A{+}1$ Gauss directions; polar angle uses Gauss nodes ($I/2$ per half-axis) or the existing three-point TY3 rule. The tensor $\langle b_a\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\Omega}^{\mathsf T}b_b\rangle/\Sigma_t$ is integrated analytically over each finite cell, and its $xy$ component is retained, so two directions in one sector that differ only in sign of $\Omega_x\Omega_y$ are not collapsed. For the mirrored quadrant-symmetric assemblies, complementary azimuths are solved once and applied by the spatial transpose.

Two reductions were verified algebraically rather than numerically. Setting `angular.node_response: true` rewrites the same weak form as a shared face-trace system, $R=\mathsf{K}_{II}^{-1}\mathsf{K}_{IB}$, $\mathsf{T}_B=\mathsf{K}_{BB}-\mathsf{K}_{BI}R$, $\mathbf{g}_B=\mathbf{f}_B-R^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{f}_I$, with the nodal interior recovered afterwards. Because $\mathsf{K}_{II}$ is block-diagonal per node, this is an exact elimination of the interior degrees of freedom. The repository checks it two ways: on a three-material rectangle, in four different spatial and angular subspaces and two of them in the reflection-invariant subspace, the condensed trace solve reproduces a direct sparse solve of the full angular matrix to $2\times10^{-11}$, and on the strong checkerboard the eliminated and direct systems give the same eigenpair to a relative tolerance of $2\times10^{-9}$ with the spatial and angular test spaces unchanged. Changing $A$, by contrast, changes the test space, and its effect is measured below. A second check concerns what a finite angular closure can and cannot achieve: for a purely absorbing slab, the map from an incident angular profile $g(\mu)$ to the exit profile is $e^{-T/\mu}g(\mu)$, which is injective on angular functions, so any fixed number of retained angular moments admits two incident profiles with identical moments and different outgoing states. A reflective slab with a cosine source gives a response coefficient of $\pi/4$ when the direction-wise solution is averaged after the fact and $3/4$ when the diffusion tensor is averaged over angle first. Exact transfer between nodes therefore requires a continuous face function, and $S$ and $M$ then only partition the phase space. This is the sense in which the closure is "strict": the algebra is exact up to the chosen finite spatial and angular spaces, and no additional face-current model is hidden in the assembly.

#### The diagonal valley of the square checkerboard.

On the one-group square checkerboard of the original paper the published PSN errors change sign along a diagonal valley whose position moves with $S$: at $I{=}30$ the strong-absorber row $S{=}2$ crosses between $M{=}4$ and $M{=}8$ ($-790.22$ pcm at $M{=}4$, $+807.92$ pcm at $M{=}8$), the $S{=}4$ row between $M{=}8$ and $M{=}12$, and the $S{=}6$ and $S{=}8$ rows between $M{=}12$ and $M{=}24$. Replacing only the node-interior diffusion coefficient by the exact sector tensor, while keeping the original four-mode body and face-average matching, does not remove the crossing ($-1363.03$ pcm and $+76.41$ pcm on the same two points), which locates the sign change in the face closure rather than in the body tensor alone.

With the sector closure the sign change is absent over the tested range. All 130 points of the four sector scans have negative eigenvalue error, and refining either axis at fixed values of the other moves the error monotonically toward zero: $A{=}0$ over $S{=}2,4,6,8,12$ with $M{=}4,8,12,24$ and $S{=}12$ also at $M{=}48,96$ for both absorbers (44 points), the same grid at $A{=}1$ in the quadratic subspace (40 points), that coefficient instead in the bilinear subspace (40 points), and $A{=}2$ at six points. The rectangular UO$_2$ matrices contain one non-monotone step that was investigated separately: moving from $M{=}2$ to $M{=}4$ makes the eigenvalue worse by $57.33$ pcm at $S{=}1$ on the uniform subinterval layout. For comparison on the identical geometry, grid and thread budget, starting cold, the legacy PSN closure at $S{=}1$, $M{=}24$ gives $-10.12$ pcm in 128.08 s and the sector closure $-54.09$ pcm in 340.68 s. One input-level effect is of the same size as these differences. OpenPSN reads the C5G7 fission spectrum as supplied, with $\sum_g\chi_g=1.00000917761$, whereas OpenMC renormalises it on read; the two eigenvalues are related by $k_\text{raw}=(\sum_g\chi_g)\,k_\text{normalised}$, which shifts the present reference by $1.0896$ pcm --- the same order as the stated target accuracy. Group-wise constant corrections of this kind are the reason the comparison is reported cell by cell rather than as a single number.

How far the remaining bias is azimuthal was estimated on the same base grid at $I{=}8$ polar nodes and $A{=}1$: the successive increments $M{=}4,8,16$ and $M{=}8,16,32$ give local azimuthal orders of $1.32$ and $1.47$, and the two windows extrapolate to $-54.64$ pcm and $-58.90$ pcm as $M\rightarrow\infty$ at fixed spatial and polar resolution. Space is not the dominant partner either --- at fixed $M{=}16$, refining the $172\times172$ graded mesh to $244\times244$ moves $\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}$ by $3.24$ pcm. On this family of meshes the residual difference is therefore limited by the intra-cell material and polar resolution rather than by the number of azimuth sectors, and the reference itself carries $3.39$--$3.91$ pcm of statistical uncertainty (block sizes of 10 to 500 batches, $1.75$ pcm for the `k_combined` estimator), which is larger than the margin any single cell of Table [6](#tab:study4-core-pcm){reference-type="ref" reference="tab:study4-core-pcm"} would have to prove.

#### Symbolic and exact checks.

Because the closure is now defined by an exact reduction, several of its components were checked without discretisation. The two-layer vacuum slab with unit optical depths and equilibrium source terms $1$ and $2$ has interface scalar flux $3[1-E_2(1)]/2$, interface net current $-1/4+E_3(1)/2$, and absorption plus leakage equal to the total source $3$; the reference implementation reproduces these in rational arithmetic with exponential integrals, and inserting a virtual interface or splitting the angular band leaves every directional response and every per-cell balance unchanged. Substituting the analytic flux into both the first-order transport equation and the even-parity second order equation leaves an identically zero symbolic residual for a mirrored rectangle with isotropic scattering, and the response coefficients of an oblique characteristic crossing two interfaces and reflecting are unchanged when extra spatial subdivisions are introduced. With the C5G7 cross sections read as exact rationals, the four-reflection homogeneous seven-group eigenvalues are $0.738214699120$ (UO$_2$) and $0.839334157439$, $1.038903851962$, $1.147587627285$ (MOX 4.3 %, 7 %, 8.7 %). For the heterogeneous quarter core, per-group weighted conservation verified with exact rational linear programming certifies $\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}<1.894$; a bracket of the same width as the target accuracy has not been obtained. These 14 exact checks (10 from this round plus the 4 slab balances above) ship as `examples/psn_strict_checks/` and run under `unittest discover` in about 2 s with `sympy` and `PyYAML`; the discretised operator, the node-response identity and the interface and symmetry properties of the geometry controls are covered by the 22 regressions in `tests/test_sector_diffusion.py`, which run in about 13 s. The exact checks constrain the operator, not the truncation error of a production mesh.

#### Scope.

The quarter-core matrix is a measured grid, not a convergence proof: its best cell still differs from the Monte Carlo reference by more than that reference's own $3.41$ pcm standard error, the rectangular MOX assembly keeps an order-of-tens-of-pcm bias at its best sampled setting, and the sector closure is more expensive than the legacy path at equal $M$. What the section establishes is narrower and checkable: with the face closure taken from the same weak form as the body term, the sign-changing valley of the square checkerboard does not appear anywhere in the 84 tested points, the error is one-signed and decreases under refinement of either axis on every tested row, and the nodal-response rewriting is an exact algebraic identity on the same discrete system.

# Discussion and scope of comparisons

This study combines implementation of the published PSN formulation, assessment of homogenised and explicit-material models, and evaluation of a separate local angular-moment nodal branch. The original PSN results and the $A{=}3$ results should be interpreted as outcomes of specified discretisations within a common software framework. In particular, the rectangular campaigns change both the angular formulation and the pin tiling, so they do not isolate the improvement due to angular order alone.

Eigenvalue accuracy and spatial-distribution accuracy provide complementary information. A configuration with small errors in both is a meaningful joint accuracy result; such a result should not be dismissed solely because the eigenvalue lies near an error valley. Nevertheless, a finite region of good agreement is different from an established refinement limit. The present parameter scans mainly describe eigenvalue behaviour, while the normalised pin-averaged fission distributions are available at the selected configurations shown; they do not constitute a complete $S\times M$ spatial-field error survey. The evidence therefore supports point-specific field accuracy, but not a claim that all joint error valleys have disappeared or that joint convergence is monotonic throughout the parameter domain.

The distinction also applies to iteration tolerances. Convergence of an algebraic iteration certifies the requested solution of the discrete system under its stopping criteria; it does not quantify the remaining transport-discretisation error. Agreement with an OpenMC estimate within its reported standard error is statistical compatibility at the stated configuration, rather than proof of a deterministic error below that uncertainty. The spatial, polar and azimuthal approximations remain part of the formulation, including the restriction of the separated nodal body on mixed derivatives.

For the local angular-moment branch, the completed quarter-core study is restricted to the $S{=}1$ angular sweep. Its results and selected spatial-field comparison are reported on that basis, without a core spatial-convergence claim. Together with the assembly and checkerboard calculations, they establish the behaviour of the implemented configurations over the reported parameter ranges. Matched computational-cost comparisons and an isolated attribution of the changes to angular order are outside the evidence provided here. The finite-sector study of Section 4 is reported on the same footing: its quarter-core matrix is a grid of measured points rather than a convergence proof, its best cell stays outside the Monte Carlo reference's own standard error, and the sector closure is the more expensive of the two paths at equal $M$.

# Conclusions

We have documented OpenPSN as a multigroup nodal transport framework containing two distinct formulations. The original branch implements the published PSN construction, including rectangular-node operators, and is supported by the reported reproduction and algebraic verification results. The additional branch is a segmentwise even-parity angular-moment formulation with a PSN-inspired separated spatial response. Its local Legendre representation belongs to the multi-$P_N$-type family; increasing its azimuthal degree to $A{=}3$ is distinct from constructing a higher streaming-operator order of the original PSN method.

The numerical assessment distinguishes pin-homogenised and explicit square-fuel models and uses the corresponding OpenMC references. The original PSN calculations give low eigenvalue and spatial-field discrepancies at selected configurations, while also exhibiting parameter sensitivity in the explicit rectangular-node study. The $A{=}3$ configuration gives eigenvalue differences of $-0.5$ pcm for UO$_2$ at $S{=}2,M{=}24$ and $-0.9$ pcm for MOX at $S{=}1,M{=}12$. The selected field comparisons at $S{=}2,M{=}24$ have normalised pin-averaged fission-rate RMS differences of $0.055\%$ and $0.075\%$, respectively; the MOX eigenvalue difference at this field-comparison point is $-9.8$ pcm. The completed quarter-core study of the local angular-moment branch reports the $S{=}1$ angular sweep, with the existing $S{=}1,M{=}8$ field comparison giving $\Delta k=-1.7$ pcm and a fission-rate RMS difference of $0.286\%$. The finite-sector closure of the even-parity equation gives one-signed checkerboard errors that decrease under refinement of either axis at every tested row, and on the explicit rectangular quarter core its complete $S\times M$ matrix keeps its smallest measured difference above the $3.41$~pcm standard error of the same-geometry Monte Carlo reference.

The checkerboard results show accurate finite-parameter configurations and remaining sensitivity to polar resolution at the reported fine-mesh points. Across the studies, these observations support the accuracy of specified configurations rather than a proof of the limiting transport solution. The original rectangular and angular-moment campaigns use different numerical tilings, so their comparison does not isolate the effect of angular order. Selected field maps likewise do not establish joint monotonic convergence over the full parameter domain, and the local angular-moment core study does not address spatial refinement. Within these limits, the work provides a documented implementation, existing verification evidence and a numerical assessment of two nodal transport formulations in a common framework.
