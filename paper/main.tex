\begin{abstract}
OpenPSN is a multigroup nodal transport framework containing an
implementation of the phase space nodal (PSN) method and a separate
local angular-moment formulation. We first document the implementation
of the published PSN closure, including rectangular material nodes, and
reproduce the reported checkerboard and BWR-bundle calculations. We then
formulate a segmentwise even-parity angular-moment variant in which
local Legendre modes in azimuth are coupled through matrix-valued second
angular moments and a separated nodal spatial response. This variant
belongs to the multi-$P_N$-type family of angular discretisations; it is
distinguished from a direct higher-order extension of the original PSN
construction. Numerical assessment uses the available checkerboard and
C5G7-based calculations, with separate references for pin-homogenised
and explicit square-fuel models. Selected assembly and quarter-core
configurations give eigenvalues within the reported statistical
uncertainty of their corresponding OpenMC references, while selected
normalised pin-averaged fission distributions show small
discrepancies. The original rectangular PSN and angular-moment campaigns
use different pin tilings, so their comparison assesses the combined
numerical configurations rather than angular order alone. For the local
angular-moment branch, the completed quarter-core study is restricted to
the $S{=}1$ angular-resolution sweep. The results document the behaviour
of the implemented formulations and the accuracy of specified
configurations without establishing a general cost advantage, joint
monotonic convergence, or the limiting transport accuracy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Two deterministic paradigms dominate light-water-reactor core calculations.
\emph{Discrete-ordinates} (SN) and \emph{method-of-characteristics} (MOC)
codes track neutrons on fine meshes and are accurate but expensive and, in the
SN case, sensitive to differencing choices. \emph{Nodal} diffusion codes
work on coarse meshes and are cheap and robust, but classical diffusion
under-predicts the transport corrections (shielding, pin-wise self
shielding) that control $\keff$ and power shape.

The \textbf{Phase Space Nodal (PSN)} method of Chao
\emph{et al.}~\cite{psn2027} bridges the two. Within each node the scalar
flux is represented by a separable hyperbolic profile in the two axes, plus a
five-term quadratic particular solution of the source; the angular flux is
reconstructed from the spatial field as
$4\pi\psi(\mathbf{r},\boldsymbol{\Omega})
=\phi(\mathbf{r})-\Sigma_t^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla\phi
(\mathbf{r})$, so its directional variation is carried by a small set of
phase-space (angular) moments with closed-form correction coefficients. The
node is eliminated to give explicit relations between surface currents and
node sources, and neighbouring nodes are coupled through those currents. The
result is a nodal code with a compact spatial representation; the present
implementation uses a sparse assembly of the node-coupling system rather than
the dense form of the original reference code.

Local angular expansions on subdomains of the direction domain
precede the present work. Ghazaie \emph{et al.} formulated a Multi-PN
approximation using separate angular expansions on subdomains and a
finite-element treatment of space~\cite{ghazaie2019mpn}; Falabino
\emph{et al.} developed a piecewise-polynomial representation of
interface angular fluxes within a short-characteristic transport
solver~\cite{falabino2022mpn}. Chao \emph{et al.} distinguish their PSN
construction from such independently selected local angular
expansions~\cite{psn2027}: in PSN, the angular structure is generated by
powers of the streaming operator acting on spatial functions governed by
diffusion-type equations, and their proposed SP3-based development
concerns higher streaming-operator powers rather than a higher polynomial
degree in azimuth.

The contributions of this work are fourfold. First, we implement the
published PSN formulation in a multigroup framework with rectangular
nodes, sparse assembly and vectorised local operators. Second, we
verify the original branch through the reported implementation checks
and the reproduction of the published checkerboard and BWR-bundle
results. Third, we document a separate segmentwise even-parity
angular-moment branch, including its matrix-valued nodal response and
its relation to the original PSN closure. Fourth, we assess the
available checkerboard and C5G7-based calculations using references
appropriate to each material and geometry model. The assessment
preserves the distinction between eigenvalue trends and spatial-field
accuracy, and between controlled comparisons and comparisons in which
both the angular formulation and pin tiling change. We do not claim a
new principle of local angular expansion or a general accuracy or
efficiency ranking against the cited methods.

The remainder is organised as follows. Section~2 describes the
formulations and their implementation: the legacy PSN formulation, the
rectangular-node implementation, and the separate segmentwise
angular-moment formulation. Section~3 presents the implementation
verification and the reproduction of the published PSN results.
Section~4 assesses the checkerboard and C5G7-based
calculations, distinguishing the pin-homogenised and explicit-material
models and the two formulations: the legacy PSN branch on the
$3\times3$ pin tiling (homogenised and explicit rectangular nodes)
and the segmentwise angular-moment variant at $A{=}3$ on the $4\times4$
fuel-split tiling, with fission-field comparisons against OpenMC. Section~5 discusses the scope
of the comparisons and the relation to prior methods; Section~6 gives
the conclusions. Appendix~A tabulates the full point-by-point
reproduction of the published results.

\section{Numerical formulations and implementation}

OpenPSN is a single software framework containing two distinct
formulations of the nodal transport problem. The first is an
implementation of the published legacy PSN construction~\cite{psn2027},
including a rectangular-node extension. The second is a separate
segmentwise even-parity angular-moment variant with a PSN-inspired
nodal response, assessed at angular order $A{=}3$. The two share the
rectangular geometry, the multi-group assembly and the outer
fission-source iteration; they differ in the angular closure and, in the
C5G7 campaigns, in the pin tiling. The software name OpenPSN does not
imply that all of its results are instances of the original PSN method.

\subsection{Legacy PSN}

The full theory is given in Ref.~\cite{psn2027}; we summarise the
ingredients needed here. The legacy PSN reconstruction combines a
spatial nodal field with a direction-dependent gradient term: the
scalar flux is a separable hyperbolic profile in space (plus the
five-term source particular solution) whose coefficients are fixed by
the node-averaged flux and a set of surface-current (moment) unknowns,
and the angular flux is reconstructed from that field, so it is \emph{not}
constant within an angular segment --- its directional variation is
constrained by derivatives of the underlying spatial field. Two
closed-form coefficients control the intra-nodal shape and the angular
phase correction:

\begin{align}
\alpha_{imn} &\quad\text{(face-flux weight, }\overline{\Phi}_{imn}
=\alpha_{imn}\,\overline{\phi}_{im}^{\,n}\text{, Eq.~(2.8a) of
Ref.~\cite{psn2027}),}\\
\beta_i  &= 1-\tfrac{1}{4}\cos\Delta\theta_i-\tfrac{1}{4}\cos 2\theta_i
          -\tfrac{1}{2}\cos 2\theta_i\,\cos\Delta\theta_i
          \quad\text{(generic phase-space coefficient, Eq.~2.8d).}
\end{align}

The closed form for $\beta_i$ in Eq.~(2.8d) is an equivalent
closed-form expression re-derived from the integral form of the
phase-space moment, and the two are algebraically identical; we
verified the two to $2.2\times10^{-16}$ and use the closed form in the
implementation. In the fine-angle limit the coefficient reduces to the
single-direction P1 closure
$(3/2)\sin^2\theta_i$.

Eliminating the node unknowns yields a sparse system coupling the
surface-current (and node-source) unknowns of neighbouring nodes, which is
iterated with a standard fission-source (power) iteration; the sparse
node-coupling system is solved directly at each outer sweep.

\subsection{Implementation and the high-precision formulation}

OpenPSN (run as \texttt{python -m psn2d run problem.yaml}) is a
pure-Python implementation (NumPy/SciPy)
accepting any number of energy groups, an arbitrary material grid with
independent per-edge reflect/vacuum boundary conditions, and a user-set
angular order $M$, spatial sub-division $S$, angular model, and
convergence tolerance.
The four implementation components are:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(\roman*)}, leftmargin=2.2em]
\item \textbf{Sparse assembly (removes the quadratic growth).}
  For the legacy PSN branch the nodal coupling matrix has only
  $\sim$9 non-zeroes per row (the node couples to its own face-current
  unknowns and to its neighbours), so the assembly scales linearly in
  the node count where the natural dense form scales quadratically. We
  assemble in coordinate (COO) format and solve in
  compressed-sparse-column (CSC) format; the path agrees with the plain
  dense reference to $10^{-15}$, and the $10{,}404$-node refinement
  ($250{,}920$ unknowns) converges to $10^{-10}$ in the
  relative eigenvalue change. The angular-moment branch assembles a
  larger per-node system ($4K$ face-mode unknowns at $A{=}3$) with the
  same sparse structure.

\item \textbf{Vectorised node operators, verified to $10^{-15}$.}
  The node-state map is strictly linear in the $(J_4,q)$ unknowns. We probe
  it with nine basis vectors once to build the $9\times9$ linear map, then
  apply it as a batched matrix multiply over all nodes and all groups.
  The vectorised path is verified identical to the validated scalar loop to
  $10^{-15}$ on five representative problems (the shipped verification
  script \texttt{verify\_vectorized\_equiv.py}): it changes no
  number of the validated solver.

\item \textbf{Rectangular nodes with per-node widths (no
  homogenisation).}
The original formulation carries a single material per node, with
no intra-node structure, so a pin cell enters either as a
homogenised node or through an explicit multi-node geometry.
OpenPSN extends the node operators to arbitrary
  per-node widths: each rectangular node carries its own width in $x$ and
  $y$, and nodes meet corner-to-corner only (no T-junctions). This lets a
  discrete fuel rod be represented \emph{explicitly} inside a nodal grid ---
  e.g.\ the C5G7 1.26~cm circular rod is replaced by an equal-area
  $s=\sqrt{\pi r^{2}}=0.95713$~cm fuel square centred on the same pitch,
  giving a $3\times3$ rectangle per pin and a globally edge-aligned tiling
  (17$\times$17 assembly $=51\times51$ nodes; 1/4 core $=153\times153$).
  Un-homogenised 7-group cross sections can then be assigned per
  rectangle (fuel vs.\ water), and the homogenisation model --- the usual
  source of nodal bias on MOX benchmarks --- is removed from the problem
  entirely. The rectangular node operators are the same closed-form
  parabolic/phase-space forms as the square case evaluated on the actual
  node widths; they are verified node-by-node against the square-node path
  (which they reduce to exactly when all widths are equal).

\item \textbf{Segmentwise angular-moment nodal variant
  (Section~\ref{sec:amvariant}).}
  The additional angular-moment branch retains $K{=}A{+}1$ local
  even-parity modes per segment. It uses a PSN-inspired nodal response,
  but changes the angular closure and the body decay scales. The
  original-scale $A{=}0$ regression branch and the directional $A{=}0$
  member must be distinguished; only the former is reported to
  reproduce the legacy state matrix, and $A{=}1,2,3$ increase the local
  angular polynomial order. Each $(i,g)$ node system is a fixed sparse
  linear system in $K$ face-mode unknowns per face ($4K$ per node), and
  every point of the assessment converges to the same $10^{-7}$ gate.
  The accuracy of the local assembly is certified against an
  independent full-mesh direct solve: at full convergence the
  eigenvalues agree to ${\le}2\times10^{-14}$ for $A{=}1,2,3$.
\end{enumerate}

These components are shared by both branches where stated: they make
the published method reproducible to machine precision and let each
branch converge to $10^{-7}$--$10^{-10}$ in relative eigenvalue change
on multi-group coarse cores, at subdivisions the dense formulation
cannot address. The physics of the two branches differs, however: the
angular-moment variant changes the angular closure, the body decay
scales and the per-node unknown count, and that change is the subject
of the assessment in Section~4 rather than an implementation
convenience.

\subsection{The segmentwise angular-moment variant}\label{sec:amvariant}

\subsubsection{The $A{=}3$ closure: projection of the even-parity
transport equation, compared with PSN}

The local angular-moment branch changes the retained angular space and
the body closure. It starts from the even-parity form of the transport
equation under the stated material and source assumptions, represents
the even field within each azimuthal segment in local Legendre modes,
and reconstructs the odd field from the even-field gradient. Its sectorwise
moment construction is classified here as multi-$P_N$-type; its use of a
PSN-inspired spatial response does not make it the original PSN closure
at a higher angular order. In particular, $A{=}3$ denotes a cubic
polynomial in local azimuth with four retained even coefficients per
segment; it does not denote global spherical-harmonic $P_3$ or
SP$_3$. The polar treatment remains the one stated for each calculation.

\emph{Where the original PSN approximates.}
Start from the steady multi-group transport equation inside a
homogeneous node of the original phase-space nodal method~\cite{psn2027},
Eq.~(2.1a) of that paper:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla\psi(\boldsymbol{\Omega},\mathbf{r})
+\Sigma_t\psi(\boldsymbol{\Omega},\mathbf{r}) = \frac{Q(\mathbf{r})}{4\pi},
\label{eq:transport}
\end{equation}
with $Q$ the isotropic (scattering $+$ fission) source and
$\phi=\int\psi\,d\Omega$. The lowest GSPN ansatz, Eq.~(2.3a) of
Ref.~\cite{psn2027},
\begin{equation}
\psi_{P_1} = \frac{1}{4\pi}\Big[\phi_0
-\frac{1}{\Sigma_t}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla\phi_0\Big],
\end{equation}
annihilates the first-derivative term when substituted back into
Eq.~\eqref{eq:transport}, leaving
$-\frac{1}{\Sigma_t}(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla)^2\phi_0
+\Sigma_t\phi_0 = Q$, which no single angle-independent
$\phi_0$ can satisfy for every direction. The original paper therefore
integrates over the full solid angle, using
$\langle\Omega_j\Omega_l\rangle_{4\pi}=\delta_{jl}/3$, and obtains the
isotropic diffusion equation, its Eq.~(2.3c),
\begin{equation}
-\frac{1}{3\Sigma_t}\nabla^2\phi_0+\Sigma_t\phi_0 = Q,
\label{eq:diff3c}
\end{equation}
whose wave number is $k^2 = 3\Sigma_t^2$ (Eq.~(2.11b)). The reconstruction
$\psi = \frac{1}{4\pi}[\phi - \Sigma_t^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla\phi]$
with $\phi=\phi_0+C$ (Eqs.~(2.3d,e)) adds one node-constant degree of
freedom fixed by the phase-space node balance (Eq.~(2.9a)), but the
\emph{body} of the node is still governed by the full-solid-angle
average of Eq.~\eqref{eq:diff3c}: the direction-dependent operator
$(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla)^2$ has been replaced by the
angle-averaged Laplacian. The face quantities, by contrast, retain the
true angular second moments: the original face current
(Eq.~(2.8b)) carries the polar factor
$\beta_i/3=\tau_i/2$ with $\beta_i=\tfrac32\,\tau_i$
(Eq.~(2.8d)) and the azimuthal
chordal-averaging factor $\gamma_m=\sin\Delta\varphi_m/\Delta\varphi_m$,
i.e.\\ a direction-dependent diffusion tensor, with
$c_m$ the segment centre,
\[
D^{(0)}_{im} = \frac{\tau_i}{2\Sigma_t}
\begin{pmatrix}
1+\gamma_m\cos 2c_m & \gamma_m\sin 2c_m\\
\gamma_m\sin 2c_m & 1-\gamma_m\cos 2c_m
\end{pmatrix}
\]
with $\tau_i=\langle\sin^2\theta\rangle_i$.
\textbf{The original
method thus evaluates the body on the isotropic scale while the faces
carry the anisotropic angular moments; the $A{=}3$ angular-moment closure
makes the body use the same angular second moments as the faces, at
every angular order.}

\emph{Parity elimination and the exact even-parity equation.}
Write $f=4\pi\psi$ and split into even and odd parity,
$e(\Omega)=\tfrac12[f(\Omega)+f(-\Omega)]$,
$o(\Omega)=\tfrac12[f(\Omega)-f(-\Omega)]$:
$\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla o+\Sigma_t e=Q$ and
$\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla e+\Sigma_t o=0$. Inside a node of
constant $\Sigma_t$ the odd parity is eliminated \emph{exactly},
$o=-\Sigma_t^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla e$, giving the
directional second-order equation
\begin{equation}
-\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla\Big(\Sigma_t^{-1}
\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla e\Big)+\Sigma_t e = Q.
\label{eq:A1}
\end{equation}
This is the mother equation of the closure. If the even-parity trial
field is restricted to be angle-independent and
Eq.~\eqref{eq:A1} is then averaged over the full solid angle,
$\langle\boldsymbol{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\Omega}\rangle_{4\pi}
=\tfrac13\mathbf{I}$, the result is Eq.~\eqref{eq:diff3c}, and the
original reconstruction (2.3a) is recovered as the exact
parity-elimination identity. Odd-parity elimination is performed \emph{before} angular
projection, so the projected streaming terms contain the \emph{full}
second angular moments $\langle b_a\,\Omega_x^2\, b_b\rangle$. If
both parities were first projected onto the same finite angular space,
eliminating the projected odd field would generally produce products of
projected first moments,
$\langle b_a\Omega_x b_c\rangle\langle b_c\Omega_x b_b\rangle$,
instead; these constructions need not coincide. This observation does
not distinguish the present equations from all multi-$P_N$
formulations: an appropriate odd space containing the streamed even
trial functions can yield the same second-order moment system after
elimination. The even-parity form is used here to define the chosen
closure, without a novelty claim for parity elimination itself. The
eliminated odd flux is reconstructable from
$\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla e$ and is not required to stay
inside the truncated angular space.

\emph{Polar reduction and the two polar moments.}
For a $z$-invariant 2-D problem the unit direction keeps its three
components $\boldsymbol{\Omega}=(\sin\theta\cos\varphi,
\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\cos\theta)$ and the operator uses
$\partial_z=0$; with even-parity pairing over opposite directions the
$z$-component drops out of every even-parity moment below, so only the
planar projection $\boldsymbol{\Omega}_\perp=(\sin\theta\cos\varphi,
\sin\theta\sin\varphi,0)$ enters. The scalar flux is the half-plane
average
$\phi=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^\pi e\,d\varphi\,d\zeta$,
$\zeta=\cos\theta$; this half-plane folding uses, beyond the even-parity
pairing, reflection symmetry of the source and the boundaries in $z$
(or an equivalent mirrored $z$-folded field) --- the isotropic-source,
vacuum/mirror 2-D configurations of this paper satisfy it, and the
even-parity pairing alone does not extend to arbitrary asymmetric
incident boundaries. Two polar moments are carried per polar sector $i$:
the folded solid-angle weight
$W_i=\int_{\theta_-}^{\theta_+}\sin\theta\,d\theta$ (normalized,
$\sum_i W_i=1$), the second moment
$\tau_i=\langle\sin^2\theta\rangle_i$ and the first moment
$\kappa_i=\langle\sin\theta\rangle_i$. With the generic even
$\theta$-partition $\Delta\theta=\pi/I$,
\begin{align}
\tau_i &= 1-\frac{\zeta_+^2+\zeta_+\zeta_-+\zeta_-^2}{3}, \\
\kappa_i &= \frac{[\zeta\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}+\arcsin\zeta]_{\zeta_-}^{\zeta_+}}
{2(\zeta_+-\zeta_-)},
\end{align}
which are exactly the original node parameters
$\tau_i=2\beta_i/3=2\Sigma_t p_i$ and $\kappa_i$
of Ref.~\cite{psn2027} (Eq.~(2.8d) for $\beta_i$; the generic polar
set --- the source paper's three-polar protocol is the same structure
with fixed quadrature nodes). These are P0
moments per polar sector --- the polar quadrature is not extended
here; $I$ stays a partition parameter.

\emph{Azimuthal projection: the $K$-mode segment space.}
The essential new degree of freedom is the intra-segment angular
representation. Within segment $m$, $\varphi\in[\varphi_-,\varphi_+]$
of width $\Delta\varphi$, centre $c_m$, half-width $\delta_m=\Delta\varphi/2$,
set $\xi=(\varphi-c_m)/\delta_m\in[-1,1]$ and expand the even-parity
angular flux in the orthonormal Legendre basis of the segment,
\begin{equation}
e(\mathbf{r},\varphi) \simeq \sum_{a=0}^{A} b_a(\xi)\,e_a(\mathbf{r}),
\qquad b_a(\xi)=\sqrt{2a+1}\,P_a(\xi),
\label{eq:azexp}
\end{equation}
with $K=A{+}1$ unknowns per face per segment ($K{=}4$ at $A{=}3$) and
$\langle f\rangle=\tfrac12\int_{-1}^{1}f\,d\xi$, so
$\langle b_a b_b\rangle=\delta_{ab}$ and $\langle b_a\rangle=\delta_{a0}$.
Projecting Eq.~\eqref{eq:A1}: multiply by each test basis function
$b_a$, average over the segment and the polar sector; the basis is
angle-space only, so the spatial derivatives come out of the angular
integrals. The $K$ test equations per segment are the Galerkin projections of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:A1} onto the segment basis; they supersede the original
single segment-mean balance (Eq.~(2.9a) of
Ref.~\cite{psn2027}) in the angular representation, without
constituting a higher streaming-operator order of the original PSN
construction.
The collision term is diagonal by orthogonality, the isotropic source
feeds only the $a{=}0$ mode, and the streaming term produces the three
segment-averaged moment matrices
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}_m{}_{ab} = \big\langle b_a b_b\cos^2\varphi\big\rangle,
\qquad
\mathbf{S}_m{}_{ab} = \big\langle b_a b_b\sin^2\varphi\big\rangle,
\qquad
\mathbf{H}_m{}_{ab} = \big\langle b_a b_b\sin\varphi\cos\varphi\big\rangle,
\label{eq:CSH}
\end{equation}
evaluated once per segment by Gauss quadrature and cached. The
matrices are symmetric, $\mathbf{C}+\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{I}_K$,
$\mathbf{C}$ and $\mathbf{S}$ positive definite for any positive-width
segment (grazing-adjacent segments can be ill-conditioned, which the
spectral construction below monitors), and $\mathbf{H}$ is generically
non-commuting with both.

\emph{The directional diffusion coefficients.}
Substituting the expansion \eqref{eq:azexp} into the mother equation
\eqref{eq:A1} and expanding the directional operator inside a node of
constant $\Sigma_t$: the in-plane projection
$\boldsymbol{\Omega}_\perp=(\sin\theta\cos\varphi,\sin\theta\sin\varphi,0)$
is independent of $(x,y)$, so the streaming term acts purely on the
spatial derivatives of the angular field,
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla\bigl(\boldsymbol{\Omega}\cdot\nabla e\bigr)
=\sin^2\theta\Bigl(\cos^2\varphi\, e_{xx}
+2\sin\varphi\cos\varphi\, e_{xy}+\sin^2\varphi\, e_{yy}\Bigr),
\label{eq:stream}
\end{equation}
where $e_{xx}$, $e_{xy}$, $e_{yy}$ are the second spatial derivatives
of $e$.

The projection then separates exactly. Multiply
\eqref{eq:stream} by the test basis $b_a$ and average over the
segment $m$ and the polar sector $i$. The collision term is diagonal
by orthogonality ($\langle b_a b_b\rangle=\delta_{ab}$), the
isotropic source feeds only the $a{=}0$ mode, and the streaming term
admits the expansion $e=\sum_b b_b\,e_b$ with the spatial
derivatives passing through the angular average unchanged. Since
$\sin^2\theta$ depends only on the polar angle, the $\theta$- and
$\varphi$-averages factorize, and the contribution of the streaming
term to the $a$-th test equation is
\begin{equation}
-\frac{\tau_i}{\Sigma_t}\sum_{b=0}^{A}\Bigl(
C_{ab}\,e_{b,xx}+2H_{ab}\,e_{b,xy}+S_{ab}\,e_{b,yy}\Bigr),
\label{eq:proj}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_i=\langle\sin^2\theta\rangle_i$ is the sector's second
polar moment and the angular integrals are exactly the moment
matrices \eqref{eq:CSH}: the three directional second derivatives
$e_{xx}$, $e_{xy}$, $e_{yy}$ select $\mathbf{C}$, $\mathbf{H}$,
$\mathbf{S}$ respectively, and no other combination enters. In vector
form, with $\mathbf{e}_{xx}=(e_{0,xx},\ldots,e_{A,xx})^T$ and
analogously for $e_{xy}$, $e_{yy}$, this is the projected body
equation
\begin{equation}
-\frac{\tau_i}{\Sigma_t}\Bigl(\mathbf{C}\,\mathbf{e}_{xx}
+2\mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{e}_{xy}+\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{e}_{yy}\Bigr)
+\Sigma_t\,\mathbf{e}=\mathbf{v}_0\,q(\mathbf{r}),
\label{eq:A7}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}=(e_0,\ldots,e_A)^T$, $\mathbf{v}_0=(1,0,\ldots,0)^T$,
and $q$ is the five-term source (below). Identifying the
\emph{directional diffusion coefficients}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{D}_x = \frac{\tau_i}{\Sigma_t}\mathbf{C} = 2p\,\mathbf{C},
\qquad
\mathbf{D}_y = \frac{\tau_i}{\Sigma_t}\mathbf{S} = 2p\,\mathbf{S},
\qquad
\mathbf{D}_{xy} = \frac{\tau_i}{\Sigma_t}\mathbf{H} = 2p\,\mathbf{H},
\label{eq:A8}
\end{equation}
with $p=\beta_i/(3\Sigma_t)=\tau_i/(2\Sigma_t)$ the original node
parameter (Eq.~(2.8d) of Ref.~\cite{psn2027} gives
$\beta_i=\tfrac32\,\tau_i$).

\textbf{The $A{=}0$ limit reproduces the original face current
exactly.} Restricting Eq.~\eqref{eq:A8} to the $a{=}0$ component and
writing $c_m$ and $\Delta\varphi_m$ for the segment centre and width,
the three scalar coefficients become
\begin{equation}
(\mathbf{D}_x)_{00} = \frac{\tau_i}{2\Sigma_t}\,
\bigl(1+\gamma_m\cos 2c_m\bigr),\qquad
(\mathbf{D}_{xy})_{00} = \frac{\tau_i}{2\Sigma_t}\,
\gamma_m\sin 2c_m,\qquad
(\mathbf{D}_y)_{00} = \frac{\tau_i}{2\Sigma_t}\,
\bigl(1-\gamma_m\cos 2c_m\bigr),
\label{eq:CSH-A0}
\end{equation}
$\gamma_m=\sin\Delta\varphi_m/\Delta\varphi_m$ as in
Eq.~(2.8b) of Ref.~\cite{psn2027}. With the face-current relation
(Section~\ref{sec:face} below),
$J_n=-(n_x\mathbf{D}_x+n_y\mathbf{D}_{xy})\bar{e}_x^{\,n}
-(n_x\mathbf{D}_{xy}+n_y\mathbf{D}_y)\bar{e}_y^{\,n}$, the $a{=}0$
component is then $J_n=-\hat{\mathbf{n}}^T D^{(0)}_{im}
\overline{\nabla\phi}^{\,n}$ with
$D^{(0)}_{im}=\frac{\tau_i}{2\Sigma_t}
\bigl[(1-\gamma_m)\mathbf{I}+2\gamma_m\mathbf{d}_m\mathbf{d}_m^T\bigr]$
($\mathbf{d}_m=(\cos c_m,\sin c_m)^T$) the original face-current
tensor, \emph{for any face normal and any segment} --- the directional face current at $A{=}0$ is the original
Eq.~(2.8b), not an approximation to it.

\textbf{The original wave number.} The scalar $k^2=3\Sigma_t^2$ of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:diff3c} is the $A{=}0$ special case of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:A8} in which both averages are taken over their full
ranges --- the azimuthal segment over the complete half plane
($\Delta\varphi_m=\pi$, $\gamma_m=0$, $(\mathbf{C})_{00}=(\mathbf{S})_{00}=\tfrac12$,
$\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{0}$) \emph{and} the polar range over the full
solid angle, $\tau_i=\tfrac23$. Then
$\mathbf{D}=\frac{\tau_i}{2\Sigma_t}\mathbf{I}=\frac{1}{3\Sigma_t}\mathbf{I}$
and the single decay constant
$k=\sqrt{\Sigma_t/\tfrac{1}{3\Sigma_t}}=\sqrt{3}\,\Sigma_t$ is
recovered. For a truncated polar range the decay rates are
$\sqrt{\Sigma_t/\lambda_j}$ with $\lambda_j$ the spectrum of
$(\tau_i/\Sigma_t)\mathbf{C}$: at $A{=}0$ over the full half-plane
that spectrum is the single value $\tau_i/(2\Sigma_t)$, so the two scales
coincide only at $\tau_i=\tfrac23$ --- a full-half-plane $A{=}0$
statement; for a finite segment the whole $\mathbf{C}$ spectrum, not
the polar moment alone, sets the rates. The angular-moment closure
generalizes that one decay constant to the spectrum of the
segment-resolved matrix; at $A{=}0$ the two discretizations still
differ, because the original \emph{body} uses a single scalar wave
number (full-solid-angle for the generic set, per-sector for the
three-polar protocol) while the directional body uses the
\emph{segment-resolved} decay rates (Section~\ref{sec:a3a0} below).

\emph{Rectangular nodes: the per-node widths enter only through the
spatial scales.}
The angular construction above is width-independent: neither the
projected equation \eqref{eq:A7} nor the moment matrices
\eqref{eq:CSH} contains a node width, and the same is true of the
original method, whose spatial shape (Eq.~(2.11a) of
Ref.~\cite{psn2027}) uses the scalar wave number
$k=\sqrt{3}\,\Sigma_t$ for the generic polar set and
$k_i=\sqrt{2}\,\Sigma_t/\mu_i$ for the three-polar protocol, with the
node widths entering only through the spatial coordinates and the
hyperbolic arguments. For rectangular nodes the source basis is
rescaled by the per-axis coordinates $\eta_x=2x/h_x$,
$\eta_y=2y/h_y$ (the same five spatial moments), and the hyperbolic
family below carries per-axis arguments $U_x=h_xK_x/2$,
$U_y=h_yK_y/2$, so each angular mode decays across the node at its own
rate $k_j=\sqrt{\Sigma_t/\lambda_j}$ over the actual width of each
axis. Nodes meet corner-to-corner only (no T-junctions), so on this
conforming grid every face has a single shared tangential length and
the face-current matching is unchanged (the two nodes on either side
of a face need not have the same width in the normal direction). When all widths are equal the rectangular
operators reduce exactly to the square-node path, and the two are
verified node-by-node to machine precision (the regression of
Section~2.2).

\emph{The body function: hyperbolic functions of the matrix spectrum.}
With the five-term spatial source
\begin{equation}
q(x,y) = q_0+q_{1x}P_1(\eta_x)+q_{1y}P_1(\eta_y)+q_{2x}P_2(\eta_x)
+q_{2y}P_2(\eta_y),\qquad
\eta_x=\frac{2x}{h_x},\quad \eta_y=\frac{2y}{h_y},
\label{eq:source5}
\end{equation}
(the same five spatial source moments as the original method; the
spatial function family is unchanged, written in the rescaled
per-axis coordinates), the even-parity body is sought in the separated
hyperbolic family
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{e}(x,y) = F_x(x)\mathbf{a}_x+G_x(x)\mathbf{b}_x
+F_y(y)\mathbf{a}_y+G_y(y)\mathbf{b}_y+\mathbf{c}
+\mathbf{v}_0\,q(x,y)/\Sigma_t,
\label{eq:A10}
\end{equation}
$F_x(x)=\cosh(K_x x)\cosh(U_x)^{-1}$,
$G_x(x)=\sinh(K_x x)\cosh(U_x)^{-1}$ (and y-analogues), with the
positive-definite square roots $K_x=(\Sigma_t\mathbf{D}_x^{-1})^{1/2}$,
$U_x=h_xK_x/2$ and similarly for $y$. Each scalar mode of the
eigen-decomposition of $\mathbf{D}_x$ contributes a hyperbolic pair at
its own decay constant $k_j=\sqrt{\Sigma_t/\lambda_j}$; the
scaled-hyperbolic form $\cosh v/\cosh u$ (all exponents non-positive)
guards the grazing directions against overflow. The quadratic source
fixes the constant vector by $\partial_x^2 P_2(\eta_x)=12/h_x^2$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{c} = \frac{12}{\Sigma_t^2}\Big(
\mathbf{D}_x\mathbf{v}_0\,\frac{q_{2x}}{h_x^2}
+\mathbf{D}_y\mathbf{v}_0\,\frac{q_{2y}}{h_y^2}\Big).
\label{eq:A11}
\end{equation}
The family has $e_{xy}=0$; it satisfies the \emph{projected} node
equation Eq.~\eqref{eq:A7} inside this separated space --- the general
mixed-derivative response is not recovered, and
$\mathbf{D}_{xy}$ acts through the face tangential-gradient current
(below), not through the body. All matrix functions of one axis
($\tanh U$, $\tanh U/U$, and the space-integrals $I_1,I_2$ defined below)
are built from the \emph{same} eigen-decomposition; in particular
$\mathbf{D}_{xy}\tanh U_y$ is not commutable and keeps that order.

\emph{Face currents: the $4K\times4K$ local system.}\label{sec:face}
With $t_x=\tanh U_x$ and the face operators $A_x=\mathbf{D}_xK_xt_x$,
$B_x=\mathbf{D}_xK_x$, $H_y=\frac{2}{h_y}\mathbf{D}_{xy}\tanh U_y$,
$H_x=\frac{2}{h_x}\mathbf{D}_{xy}\tanh U_x$ (y-analogues of $A_x,B_x$),
the outward face-current coefficients of the four faces satisfy the
block relation
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{J}_{x+}\\\mathbf{J}_{x-}\\
\mathbf{J}_{y+}\\\mathbf{J}_{y-}\end{pmatrix}}_{\mathbf{J}}
= \underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}
-A_x&-B_x&0&-H_y\\
-A_x& B_x&0& H_y\\
0&-H_x&-A_y&-B_y\\
0& H_x&-A_y& B_y
\end{pmatrix}}_{\mathcal{K}}
\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{a}_x\\\mathbf{b}_x\\\mathbf{a}_y\\\mathbf{b}_y\end{pmatrix}
+ \mathcal{O}\,\mathbf{q},
\label{eq:A13}
\end{equation}
which is the tensor face-current relation
$\mathbf{J}_n = -\big(n_x\mathbf{D}_x+n_y\mathbf{D}_{xy}\big)\overline{\mathbf{e}}_x^{\,n}
-\big(n_x\mathbf{D}_{xy}+n_y\mathbf{D}_y\big)\overline{\mathbf{e}}_y^{\,n}$:
\emph{every angular mode sees its own directional diffusion
coefficient}, which is the origin of the name. The source-offset block
$\mathcal{O}$ is the face evaluation of the five-term particular
solution, with an overall $1/\Sigma_t$ factor: its rows are the
columns $\mathbf{D}_x\mathbf{v}_0$, $\mathbf{D}_y\mathbf{v}_0$,
$\mathbf{D}_{xy}\mathbf{v}_0$ scaled by $(1/\Sigma_t)(2/h)$ and
$(1/\Sigma_t)(6/h)$, so \emph{high angular modes do receive source
contribution} even though the source itself is purely $a{=}0$.
Inverting Eq.~\eqref{eq:A13} (by direct solve, not explicit
inversion) yields the local response mapping
$\mathbf{q}$ and $\mathbf{J}\mapsto$ face means and the five spatial
moments. At $K{=}4$ the local coefficient system is $16\times16$ per
node (plus the five source columns); the angular quadrature points used
to build Eq.~\eqref{eq:CSH} are construction points, not transport
unknowns. The five spatial moments returned to the source update are
\begin{align}
m_0 &= \mathbf{v}_0^T\overline{\mathbf{e}}, \qquad
m_{1x} = 3\,\mathbf{v}_0^T I_1(U_x)\,\mathbf{b}_x + q_{1x}/\Sigma_t,\\
m_{2x} &= 5\,\mathbf{v}_0^T I_2(U_x)\,\mathbf{a}_x + q_{2x}/\Sigma_t,
\end{align}
with $I_1(U)=U^{-1}(I-U^{-1}\tanh U)$, $I_2(U)=U^{-1}(\tanh U-3I_1(U))$
and y-analogues. The angular-zeroth extraction is performed
\emph{after} the full matrix--vector product (e.g.\
$(3\,I_1\mathbf{b}_x)_0$, not $3(I_1)_{00}(\mathbf{b}_x)_0$): high
angular modes feed the scalar source through the off-diagonal entries,
which is exactly the coupling that the single-mode closure cannot
represent. The node volume balance
$\overline{\mathbf{e}}=\mathbf{v}_0 q_0/\Sigma_t
-(\mathbf{J}_{x+}+\mathbf{J}_{x-})/(h_x\Sigma_t)
-(\mathbf{J}_{y+}+\mathbf{J}_{y-})/(h_y\Sigma_t)$ holds for every
angular mode simultaneously.

\emph{Assembly: face fluxes, boundaries, and the eigenproblem.}
The signed face flux entering the global connection is the original
PSN convention with the basis promoted to a matrix:
$\Phi_n = \frac{\kappa_i}{2}\mathbf{E}_n\,\mathbf{L}_n$,
$\big(\mathbf{E}_n\big)_{ab}=\langle b_a b_b\cos(\varphi-\varphi_n)\rangle$,
with $\mathbf{L}_n$ the face-mean even-parity vector,
$\big(\mathbf{L}_n\big)_a = \frac{1}{|\partial n|}\int_{\partial n}
e_a\,ds$; at $A{=}0$ this is exactly the original $\alpha\,\times$
face-mean. The internal face closure uses the same raw face-mean
continuity and anti-symmetric outward-current pairing as the original
method; on a uniform even $M$ grid with axis-aligned nodes every
segment has a single sign of $\Omega\cdot n$, so the connection is
exact in this $L$-representation. Mirror reflection maps segment $m$ to
$M{-}1{-}m$ with local $\xi\mapsto-\xi$, which in the $K$-mode
representation is $L_m = R\,L_{m'}$, $J_m = -R\,J_{m'}$ with
$R=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,1,-1,\ldots)$ --- odd modes reverse their
reflection sign; the moment matrices obey the corresponding identities
$C_{m'}=RC_mR$, $H_{m'}=-RH_mR$. Vacuum is the hemispheric current
$J = B\,L$ with $B$ the positive-definite matrix
$\bigl(B\bigr)_{ab}=\kappa_i\langle b_a b_b
|\cos(\varphi-\varphi_n)|\rangle$ (the same normal-relative form on
every face: $\varphi_n=0$ on $x$ faces, $\pi/2$ on $y$ faces), $\kappa_i$
the first polar
moment (not $\sqrt{\tau_i}$); at $A{=}0$ this is
$B_{00}=2|\alpha_{imn}|$, the original vacuum condition. Multi-group assembly keeps the original structure: each
group's five-moment response $R_g$ is coupled through the isotropic
scattering and fission blocks, the discrete eigenproblem is non-symmetric
and is driven by the same outer fission-source (power) iteration as the
original implementation; each outer sweep solves the node-coupling
system with a Krylov method (GMRES). The outer iteration stops when the
relative eigenvalue change $|\Delta k|/k$ and the fission-source
iterate change both fall below $10^{-7}$: the iterate change is the
$\ell_2$ norm of the difference between successive fission sources
divided by the larger of the previous source's norm and unity,
$r_F=\|s^{(n+1)}-s^{(n)}\|_2/\max(\|s^{(n)}\|_2,1)$, a scaled
iterate change --- relative when the source norm exceeds unity,
absolute below it --- with both iterates produced on the same
normalisation convention; the GMRES inner solve runs to a relative
linear-residual tolerance of $10^{-7}$ (absolute tolerance zero) --- the
same value as the outer gate, as used by the production sweep driver;
the library default of $10^{-11}$ applies to standalone CLI runs ---
with a restart length of $20$ inner iterations, at most $800$ restart
cycles per linear system (the $\mathtt{maxiter}$ interface parameter of the
scipy implementation as invoked). This
linear-system tolerance is separate from the outer stopping
criteria.

\paragraph{Relation between legacy PSN and the directional $A{=}0$ member}
\label{sec:a3a0}
At $A{=}0$ the two branches of the implementation share the
\emph{face-current constitutive law} --- the $(0,0)$ entries of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:CSH} are the original segment means
$\langle\cos^2\varphi\rangle_m$ etc. --- and the interface
flux/current connection. The \emph{body} differs. The original branch
keeps the published scalar wave number, $k=\sqrt{3}\,\Sigma_t$ for the
generic polar set and $k_i=\sqrt{2}\,\Sigma_t/\mu_i$
($\mu_i=\sin\theta_i$) for the three-polar protocol, while the
directional branch evaluates its hyperbolic functions at the
segment-resolved spectrum of $\mathbf{D}_x,\mathbf{D}_y$, i.e.\ at
$\sqrt{\Sigma_t/\lambda_j}$ with $\lambda_j$ the segment-dependent
eigenvalues. A shared constitutive law and connection do not make the
condensed nodal response $J,q\mapsto L,m$ identical --- the body wave
number enters the face-current block matrices directly --- so the two
discretizations agree on the face current at equal gradients but not
inside the node: this is precisely the body/face scale split of the
original method (Section above) carried into the new branch, and it is
why the directional $A{=}0$ solution is a distinct, segment-resolved
discretization rather than a reproduction of the legacy state matrix
(verified numerically over all 2016 node-response configurations of a
C5G7 pin cell: 3 polar points $\times$ 7 groups $\times$ 24 azimuthal
segments $\times$ 4 material groups: the original branch at $A{=}0$
reproduces the legacy node response to $4.8\times10^{-13}$, while the
directional branch at $A{=}0$ differs by $\mathcal{O}(1)$ in the body
scales, with a decay-rate spread of $0.62$--$45.9$ across the
half-plane segments).
The $A{=}0$ directional solution is the correct limiting member of the
new family; the $A{=}1,2,3$ results below are reported with it as the
reference closure.

\section{Implementation verification and reproduction}

\subsection{Local algebra and the $A{=}0$ regression}

\paragraph{Verification of the local construction.}
Before any benchmark was re-run, the local node response was verified
against independently constructed solutions: for $A{=}0\ldots3$, five
azimuthal segment widths, both aspect ratios of a $2$-cm node, and all
five unit sources (plus one mixed source), a spatial field is built
from arbitrary homogeneous coefficients and source, the four face
flux/current vectors and the five spatial moments are computed by
independent numerical integration, and compared against the local
response matrix. Maximum normalized differences over 240 cases:
face response and five moments $4.5\times10^{-12}$, constant-vector
recovery from the volume balance $8.5\times10^{-12}$, strong residual
of the projected body equation $1.0\times10^{-15}$. These verify the
discretization \emph{as written}; they are not a transport-accuracy
statement.

These checks concern the implementation of the specified local responses
and discrete systems. Agreement between two algebraic solution paths
supports implementation consistency for those systems; it does not remove
their angular or spatial approximation errors. Likewise, the
original-scale regression establishes recovery of the legacy
implementation under that setting, rather than equivalence of the
directional $A{=}0$ closure and original PSN.

\section{Numerical assessment}

\subsection{Configuration}

C5G7 is the OECD Nuclear Energy Agency (NEA) mixed-oxide (MOX)
benchmark~\cite{nea2003,smith2004}, the standard test for uranium-oxide
(UOX)/MOX inter-cell transport: 17$\times$17-pin fuel assemblies on a
1.26~cm pin pitch (UO$_2$ plus three MOX enrichments), guide tubes and
fission chambers, in a 7-group structure. (The C5G7 family was later extended
to time-dependent calculations~\cite{c5g7td}; we solve the steady 2-D
problem.) We solve the \textbf{C5G7-2D 1/4-core} problem: a 2$\times$2 block
of fuel assemblies (UO2 on the diagonal, MOX on the anti-diagonal) plus an
L-shaped one-assembly water reflector, 51$\times$51 pins, 64.26~cm on a side.
Following Ref.~\cite{nea2003} we apply \emph{vacuum} boundaries on the two
open (right, top) edges and \emph{reflective} boundaries on the two symmetry
(left, bottom) edges. We model the pin cell in three ways. \emph{(i)} The standard
pin-homogenised nodal model folds each fuel pin cell into a single
homogenised node (fuel$+$water; pure water for reflector and guide
pins). \emph{(ii)} The rectangular-node model of Section~2.2 represents
each pin by multiple homogeneous material rectangles: a $3\times3$
base tiling per pin (one fuel square plus eight water strips), so the
explicit square-fuel model resolves the fuel--moderator boundary across
nodes rather than assigning both materials to a single node; the
equal-area replacement of the circular fuel by a square remains a
geometry approximation. \emph{(iii)} The local angular-moment campaign
uses a $4\times4$ tiling obtained by splitting the fuel region into
four rectangles ($2\times2$ fuel sub-nodes). The original rectangular PSN
campaign and the angular-moment campaign thus use the same square-fuel
boundary but different numerical meshes. The 7-group cross sections
are the published C5G7 multi-group set in all cases.

Eigenvalue differences in the present tables are reported as
$\Delta k=10^5(k-k_{\mathrm{ref}})$, following the convention used for the
reported numerical data; this quantity is distinct from the relative
eigenvalue change used to stop an iteration. References for the
pin-homogenised model and the explicit square-fuel model are kept
separate, and comparisons with the circular-fuel literature reference
additionally contain a difference between geometry models.

Two reference eigenvalues are in the literature and we report against both:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\keff^{\mathrm{MCNP}}=1.18655$ ($\pm0.008\%$), the 2-D multi-group
  MCNP value in the original NEA report~\cite{nea2003,smith2004};
\item $\keff=1.18646$ (1.186456 to the printed digit), the 2-D
  resolved-fuel-pin high-fidelity value of McGraw \emph{et
  al.}~\cite{mcgraw2014}, quoted as the reference in the INL Rattlesnake
  convergence study~\cite{rattlesnake}, which explicitly notes that the
  benchmark-report value 1.18655 is $\sim$10~pcm high and converges to
  $\keff=1.186446$ ($\sim$1~pcm from the McGraw reference).
\end{itemize}
We report all deviations against 1.18646 (the more accurate high-fidelity
value). The original NEA report tabulated relative errors against its MCNP
reference 1.18655; re-referenced to 1.18646, those values shift by
$\sim$9~pcm.

\subsection{Study I --- the pinwise-homogenised nodal model}

The standard nodal treatment folds each pin's fuel and moderator into a
single homogenised node. We follow that model here so that the only
discretisation knobs left are the angular order $M$ and the spatial
sub-division $S$, and so that the result can be compared against a Monte
Carlo run on the \emph{same} homogenised data.

\paragraph{Pinwise homogenisation.}
The homogenised 7-group cross sections are built pin by pin with the
\textbf{flux-weighted self-shielding} recipe: an infinite-medium pin
cell ($r=0.54$~cm, 1.26~cm pitch) is solved with an explicit fuel/water
MOC calculation, and the fuel-group cross sections are weighted by the
self-shielded infinite-medium flux (scattering by the source-group flux).
Guide tubes are area-weighted. This is the same self-shielding-aware
homogenisation that nodal practice uses in production; it removes most of
the resonance self-shielding error that a plain area-weighted average
leaves behind.

\paragraph{Monte Carlo reference, computed directly.}
Rather than lean on a published reference for the homogenised model, we
compute the reference ourselves with OpenMC~\cite{openmc2015} on the exact
same pinwise-homogenised cross sections, so that the two sides differ only
in the transport method. Each calculation uses 20000 active batches of
100000 particles (collision estimator); the eigenvalue is the
mean over the last 20000 batches and its standard error is
$\sigma_{\mathrm{batch}}/\sqrt{N}$, which we audit to be $\approx$3.4--3.5
pcm. We run the single UO$_2$ assembly, the single MOX assembly and the 1/4
core, each with \emph{homogenised} (pin-node) and, where needed,
\emph{explicit} (resolved fuel/water) geometry. The three
\emph{homogenised} runs are the reference for the pinwise-homogenised PSN
sweep below:
\begin{itemize}
\item single UO$_2$ assembly: $1.3336285$ ($\pm3.5$~pcm),
\item single MOX assembly: $1.1853993$ ($\pm3.3$~pcm),
\item 1/4 core: $1.1868230$ ($\pm3.4$~pcm).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{OpenPSN $S\times M$ sweep.}
We sweep $S\in\{1,\dots,6\}$ and $M\in\{2,4,8,12,16,24\}$ on all
three geometries (nodes $(17S)^2$ for both assemblies, $(51S)^2$ for the
core), with the pinwise-homogenised C5G7 cross sections, and converge
every point to $10^{-10}$ in the relative eigenvalue change --- all 108
points of
Table~\ref{tab:bww}. The largest system (core, $S{=}6$,
$93{,}636$ nodes) is solved with the sparse direct factorisation
shipped with OpenPSN, verified to $10^{-15}$ against the plain
reference path.

\subsection{Study II --- the rectangular-node model with explicit
fuel/moderator rectangles}

\paragraph{Motivation and layout.}
Study I isolates the discretisation error of the nodal PSN model, but its
eigenvalue is still anchored to whatever homogenisation model feeds it. To
remove that model from the problem we use the rectangular-node extension
of Section~2.2: the circular C5G7 fuel rod ($r=0.54$~cm) is replaced by an
equal-area fuel square of side $s=\sqrt{\pi r^{2}}=0.95713$~cm centred on
the same 1.26~cm pitch, so that every pin cell becomes a $3\times3$ array
of rectangles (one fuel square plus eight water strips) and the whole core
is a globally edge-aligned rectangular tiling. Figure~\ref{fig:layout}
shows the layout. The pin pitch, the rod positions (including Gd pins,
which are the centre rectangle of their $3\times3$ block) and the total
fuel area are unchanged; the layout was verified node by node --- all
2601 pin-cell centre rectangles of the 1/4 core coincide with the 2601 pin
cells of the homogenised grid, and the total area reproduces
$4129.35~\mathrm{cm}^2$ exactly. Un-homogenised 7-group
cross sections (fuel, water, Gd, MOX) are then assigned directly to the
rectangles. Two discretisation knobs are now open: the angular order $M$
and the uniform spatial subdivision $S$ (Section~2.2), and we sweep both.

\paragraph{The slender water gap.}
The $S$-saturation pattern above points to a geometric cause. In the
$3\times3$ tiling the water-gap nodes are intrinsically slender: each edge
strip node measures $s \times w = 0.9571\times0.1514$~cm, an aspect
ratio of $6.3{:}1$ (the corner nodes, $w\times w$, are square but carry
little physics). The uniform $S$ subdivision divides \emph{both}
dimensions by $S$, so every sub-node inherits the parent's aspect ratio ---
$S$-subdivision shrinks the strip size and optical thickness but never
reshapes the strips. The residual eigenvalue bias of
Table~\ref{tab:rectsm} is therefore plausibly anchored in the strip
nodes; within the tested range, $S$-subdivision alone does not remove
the deviation from the reference, which is consistent with
the UO$_2$ rows that degrade smoothly and monotonically as $S$ grows at
fixed $M$ (weak water coupling: subdivision adds nodes but never
re-shapes the strips), the MOX rows whose only relief is a single
subdivision --- best $S{=}2$, degrading again for $S{\ge}3$ (strong
fuel--water coupling in the gap) --- and the 1/4 core, whose $S{=}2$ row
is uniformly worse than $S{=}1$ across all $M$.


\paragraph{Performance at $M{=}24$, $S{=}3$ on the rectangular core.}
This configuration has $459\times459=210{,}681$ nodes and the same
material geometry and pure seven-group cross sections as the
rectangular OpenMC reference. OpenPSN uses a diffusion-preconditioned
Ritz projection and a shared sparse Cholesky preconditioner. The
non-diagonal Cholesky entries use column-scaled 16-bit integers and
variable-length row deltas. The diagonal, matrix-vector products and
residual checks use double precision. Each angular sweep assembles and
reduces one azimuth at a time.

At a budget of 60 parallel units, the median fine-grid transport sweep
time for sweeps 2--11 decreased from 160.16~s with the earlier hybrid
incomplete-Cholesky/PCG implementation to 19.17~s, corresponding to a
speedup of \textbf{$8.35\times$ per sweep}. The hybrid run reached the
45-minute time limit after 11 sweeps. The standard CLI run completed 22 sweeps
including one independent verification sweep in 1261.48~s end to end.
Factor and operator setup took 691.31~s.
Peak host memory was 28.83~GiB and the compressed factors and
double-precision operator pool occupied 9.419~GiB.

The converged eigenvalue is $\keff=1.186767156487$.
The same-geometry OpenMC reference is $1.187217554586$
(one standard error of $3.41\pcm$), giving a signed difference of
$-45.04\pcm$ under the convention $\Delta k=10^{5}(\keff-k_\mathrm{ref})$.
The independently recomputed fine-grid relative source residual is
$2.57\times10^{-12}$. The reported relative eigenvalue residual is
$9.43\times10^{-12}$, the maximum of the last Ritz eigenvalue change
and the verification difference. The example directory includes the
timed sweep series and the configuration used for this comparison.

\section{Discussion and scope of comparisons}

This study combines implementation of the published PSN formulation,
assessment of homogenised and explicit-material models, and evaluation
of a separate local angular-moment nodal branch. The original PSN
results and the $A{=}3$ results should be interpreted as outcomes of
specified discretisations within a common software framework. In
particular, the rectangular campaigns change both the angular
formulation and the pin tiling, so they do not isolate the improvement
due to angular order alone.

Eigenvalue accuracy and spatial-distribution accuracy provide
complementary information. A configuration with small errors in both is
a meaningful joint accuracy result; such a result should not be
dismissed solely because the eigenvalue lies near an error valley.
Nevertheless, a finite region of good agreement is different from an
established refinement limit. The present parameter scans mainly
describe eigenvalue behaviour, while the normalised pin-averaged
fission distributions are available at the selected configurations
shown; they do not constitute a complete $S\times M$ spatial-field
error survey. The evidence therefore supports point-specific field
accuracy, but not a claim that all joint error valleys have
disappeared or that joint convergence is monotonic throughout the
parameter domain.

The distinction also applies to iteration tolerances. Convergence of an
algebraic iteration certifies the requested solution of the discrete
system under its stopping criteria; it does not quantify the remaining
transport-discretisation error. Agreement with an OpenMC estimate
within its reported standard error is statistical compatibility at the
stated configuration, rather than proof of a deterministic error below
that uncertainty. The spatial, polar and azimuthal approximations
remain part of the formulation, including the restriction of the
separated nodal body on mixed derivatives.

For the local angular-moment branch, the completed quarter-core study
is restricted to the $S{=}1$ angular sweep. Its results and selected
spatial-field comparison are reported on that basis, without a core
spatial-convergence claim. Together with the assembly and checkerboard
calculations, they establish the behaviour of the implemented
configurations over the reported parameter ranges. Matched
computational-cost comparisons and an isolated attribution of the
changes to angular order are outside the evidence provided here.

\section{Conclusions}

We have documented OpenPSN as a multigroup nodal transport framework
containing two distinct formulations. The original branch implements
the published PSN construction, including rectangular-node operators,
and is supported by the reported reproduction and algebraic
verification results. The additional branch is a segmentwise
even-parity angular-moment formulation with a PSN-inspired separated
spatial response. Its local Legendre representation belongs to the
multi-$P_N$-type family; increasing its azimuthal degree to $A{=}3$ is
distinct from constructing a higher streaming-operator order of the
original PSN method.

The numerical assessment distinguishes pin-homogenised and explicit
square-fuel models and uses the corresponding OpenMC references. The
original PSN calculations give low eigenvalue and spatial-field
discrepancies at selected configurations, while also exhibiting
parameter sensitivity in the explicit rectangular-node study. The
$A{=}3$ configuration gives eigenvalue differences of $-0.5$~pcm for
UO$_2$ at $S{=}2,M{=}24$ and $-0.9$~pcm for MOX at $S{=}1,M{=}12$. The
selected field comparisons at $S{=}2,M{=}24$ have normalised
pin-averaged fission-rate RMS differences of $0.055\%$ and
$0.075\%$, respectively; the MOX eigenvalue difference at this
field-comparison point is $-9.8$~pcm. The completed quarter-core study
of the local angular-moment branch reports the $S{=}1$ angular sweep,
with the existing $S{=}1,M{=}8$ field comparison giving
$\Delta k=-1.7$~pcm and a fission-rate RMS difference of
$0.286\%$.

The checkerboard results show accurate finite-parameter
configurations and remaining sensitivity to polar resolution at the
reported fine-mesh points. Across the studies, these observations
support the accuracy of specified configurations rather than a proof
of the limiting transport solution. The original rectangular and
angular-moment campaigns use different numerical tilings, so their
comparison does not isolate the effect of angular order. Selected
field maps likewise do not establish joint monotonic convergence over
the full parameter domain, and the local angular-moment core study does
not address spatial refinement. Within these limits, the work provides
a documented implementation, existing verification evidence and a
numerical assessment of two nodal transport formulations in a common
framework.
